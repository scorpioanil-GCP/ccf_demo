Rung 10 · Tier 3

The signal is the disagreement, and every average destroys it

A corporate bond's spread is three things added together: the Treasury benchmark, the option-adjusted spread of the rating band the bond was issued into, and whatever is left — the issuer-specific residual. When the whole high-yield market widens, the first two move and the third does not. A product that serves only the total reports that as a deterioration. Measured on this estate, over one seven-week window, 22 of the 30 rated obligors would have been flagged that way.

Meanwhile the internal grade is reviewed quarterly, so it sits on a clock ninety times slower than the spread. A single product-level freshness figure over both would be actively misleading, which is why each series carries its own — and why the spread and the grade are separate entity classes, since the reference debt scorer types an observation by its class.

The unit

A bond is what trades; an obligor is what gets graded

This was found at Gate 2, against the approved specification's own headline figure, and Gate 1 was reopened for it. The first implementation averaged an obligor's instruments before detecting anything. Celanese has two bonds whose median residuals are 29.015 and 29.538 bp — normally indistinguishable. On 2024-12-11 the five-year reaches 8.039× its own median and the twenty-five-year reaches 1.27×. Averaging first halves the peak and erases the term structure entirely.

So the instrument is the primary observation unit. The obligor-level figure survives as a derived summary that never travels without its dispersion — because a mean over instruments that may be pricing different things reads, on the page, exactly like an observation. Measured: 5,267 (obligor, date) pairs in this estate carry one bond at more than twice the other.

The grade comparison stays at obligor level, and the asymmetry is deliberate: the product compares bond-market signals against obligor-level credit assessment and does not invent a per-bond grade to make the units match.

Run 1 — the confirmation. The five-year peaks at 233.245 bp on 2024-12-11, 8.039× its own median, with the grade BB in force. The credit file moves 96 days later: BB → BB-, off-cycle, and the trigger says so in its own words — “ad hoc credit review following sustained spread widening in the obligor's traded debt.” The assertion was booked four days after it took effect, and the product reports that lag as a figure.

Run 2 — a standing disagreement. Sabre's bond peaks at 282.633 bp, 7.004× its own median. The grade is B at the peak and B at the window's end, through two later reviews. The product does not say “no confirmation”; it says outcome: unchanged, names the grade that stood throughout and counts the reviews that left it alone. An earlier revision returned the same empty answer here as for an obligor carrying no grade at all — so somebody looked twice and disagreed with the market was indistinguishable from nobody has looked.

Run 3 — the market move that is not a deterioration. Over 2025-02-18 → 2025-04-07 every band widened together: B +165.0 bp, BB +150.5, BBB +51.2, A +36.0, AA +28.9. This obligor's headline moved +166.6 bp and its residual moved +1.5 bp. All three signals agree nothing happened, and the product reports no episode.

The freshness defect

History is evidence, not staleness

This product keeps years of daily quotes on purpose — that history is how a migration is detected at all. The SDK's reference scorer took the mean freshness over every observation in the view, so the archive's length became the freshness figure.

observations in the view   rsm_spread 996 · rsm_grade 9 · rsm_instrument 2 · rsm_obligor 1
observations entering F    rsm_spread   2 · rsm_grade 1 · rsm_instrument 2 · rsm_obligor 1

F over the leading edge   0.664139      F over every observation   0.004750
D_c 0.3651, signal ok                   D_c 0.9955, fail_closed
This was an SDK gap, not an example problem

The example stopped rather than supplying a local scorer. D-IMPL-333 lets a λc row declare freshness_over: leading-edge plus a series_key_attribute, and F now ranges over the newest quote per bond at or before as_of, plus the grade in force. Everything older stands behind it as the record of how the answer got there.

And supersede-only was tested first, because it looked like the existing answer. It is not: over nine observations one day apart it gives F per observation [1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1], mean 1.000000. It means this class does not decay — every historical point still enters F, now each reading as perfectly fresh. Curve-mean understates freshness by counting history as stale; supersede-only overstates it by declaring history evergreen. Neither says “the newest quote for this bond is two days old.”

The evidence

Twenty-four gates, and the two that were green when they should not have been

Every gate was sabotaged and watched go red. Twenty-one went red on their own assertion. Three could not be falsified from this deployment at all, because an SDK guard refuses the bad state before the deployment can produce an artifact — those are recorded as unavailable, never folded into passed, because they mean different things.

Two gates passed under their own sabotage on the first attempt, and both are recorded as such. RSM-G13 asserted a booking lag computed from the source record rather than from the two instants actually served — so a payload collapsing validFrom and assertedAt left the lag correct and the gate green. RSM-G23 asserted only against an obligor still in its issue band, where the grade-derived band and the instrument band are the same value — so the contamination it exists to refuse was invisible. Neither was a weak sabotage; both were weak tests.

A gate asserted only where two answers coincide proves nothing about which one produced them

RSM-G23 now also asserts against 5493004HBTOTE9DI9P12 — measured as one of the two obligors in thirty that have left their issue band. Its bonds were issued into BBB; it is now graded BB+, whose band is BB. A band derived from the grade is visibly the wrong one there, and the gate goes red.

What to change first

Three edits, in the order you will want them

  • divergence_multiple — a multiple of each bond's own median, never an absolute basis-point figure. 100 bp means one thing at AA and another at B, and one thing on a two-year and another on a twenty-five-year. Lower it and more bonds produce episodes; raise it and the anchor's 8× survives while the sibling's 1.3× still does not. That is the fastest way to see why the baseline has to be per-subject.
  • The two half_life_seconds rows — watch D_c move, and watch the ninety-fold spread stop being expressible the moment you give both classes the same rate.
  • granularity — resampling takes the last real observation in each bucket. It never interpolates, because across a grade change interpolation invents intermediate grades that never existed.

What this does not claim

An honest basis, reported rather than corrected

The market leg is netted on the band the bond was issued into, and that band does not follow the obligor's grade. Two of the thirty rated obligors have left theirs, so for them the residual is less precise — in a knowable direction, since a wider current band means the market component is understated and the residual overstated. The product flags them and does not correct them.

The correction that would destroy the product

Inferring a replacement band from the internal grade would make the market component a function of the grade the divergence is measured against. A residual computed that way could never disagree with the grade — and the disagreement is the entire product. So the residual is served as the residual on a stated basis, qualified where the obligor has migrated, and never as unqualified “pure issuer deterioration”.

No agency ratings and no TRACE data anywhere, and both were verified rather than assumed: no agency column exists in any of the estate's 118 tables, the scale is synthetic — internal, instrument identifiers are syn-i-*, and the issuer-specific figure is a residual rather than an observed trade. An SDK example ships with the SDK and is copied by adopters, so licensed values in one would be redistribution exactly as in a product.

Illustrative calibration. This example runs against a point-in-time demonstration estate rather than continuously refreshed production data. Freshness half-lives and related thresholds are calibrated to make the governed behavior observable in that snapshot. They demonstrate how the SDK applies these controls and should not be interpreted as recommended production coverage-policy thresholds.
