Orientation

Total exposure is not a sum

A credit officer testing a facility against a group limit asks one question: what do we actually have on this name? The flat data product answers it by adding facility balances keyed to the borrower's legal-entity identifier. On the demonstration anchor that returns 145,000,000.

The family owes {{gross}}. The naive answer understates by 69.8× — and it does not look wrong. It is a well-formed figure of a plausible size for a mid-cap borrower, returned without error, and nothing in it signals that a graph the product should have traversed was never traversed.

The error runs in both directions at once. A borrower-keyed sum double-counts where a guarantee has already captured the risk at the parent, and it under-counts where a cross-default binds in an entity holding no facility at all. Neither shows up as a failure; both show up as a number.

One walk, four predicate types, both directions

Ownership, ultimate consolidation, guarantee and cross-default in a single traversal — not one walk per edge type merged afterwards. At the declared depth of 3 the family is {{family}} members: 21 at depth 1, 49 at depth 2. Of the {{family}}, 19 carry an identifier in the ledger and 58 do not, and only 5 hold a facility at all. The other 72 are family by ownership, guarantee or cross-default and hold nothing — which is exactly why this is a traversal product and not a query.

The family reaches outside the anchor's ownership group entirely. Amphenol, Celanese, Avis Budget and XPO all arrive at depth 3, bound in by cross-default rather than by ownership. No ownership-only walk finds them. No borrower-keyed query finds them either.

The governed context

And why the fabric will not attest to it

Against this estate the outstanding balances are 324–396 hours old. This product's fastest input is the outstanding balance, whose half-life is four hours, because drawdowns and repayments land intraday. Context Debt comes out at 1.000 with signal fail_closed, and the class's own tolerance of {{tolerance}} is exceeded.

So the fabric assembles the product, passes every validation gate, writes the decision record — and declines to attest that the result is fit to act on.

Withholding is a refusal of fulfillment, not of response

The run still exits 0 and still answers. What the refused caller receives is the refusal's disclosed shell: the kind, the tolerance that was not met, the authority that set it, and the replay reference. What it does not carry is the determination.

Nothing was calibrated to make it serve. Not the half-life, not the evidence, not the debt calculation. A four-hour window widened to fit older demonstration data would make every later run lie about how current its money figures are.

The TTL anchor flips at this rung, and the flip is the teaching

Rungs 02 and 04 anchor on entity freshness. An exposure has no underlying entity whose validity period governs it: a facility has an effective date and a maturity, a family edge has an assertion time, but a rollup is not a fact about an entity — it is a fact about a question asked at an instant. So expires_at is request time plus four hours, no observation timestamp is read, and no latest-versus-earliest basis has to be chosen. The two earlier rungs both had to make that decision; this one does not, and the absence of the decision is the point.

The cost is real and is stated rather than hidden: the product cannot be refreshed in place and cannot be served past expiry. A rollup found in a queue after its window has closed is refused on read, and that refusal is correct behavior rather than an incident.

Every determination is replayed — served or withheld

The decision record is written before the response is released, through the same writer, the same store and the same artifact class on both branches. A separate class for withheld determinations would be exactly the second-class replay D-IMPL-253 Q13 forbids: “Refusal is itself a decision and deserves the same five-dimensional replay model.”

Measured, the withheld record is larger than the served one — 20,075 bytes against 16,873 — because it additionally carries the refusal envelope, its evidence and the debt breakdown. All five dimensions are populated on both. They differ in exactly one place: dimension 5 carries a product content address when served, and no-product-address with refusal_recorded: true when withheld.

The snapshot

Both transcripts are committed artifacts of runs at a pinned instant, built from the tree at commit {{commit}}, anchored on {{anchor}}, asked as of {{asked_at}}. The page is generated from those transcripts and never from a live run. At a four-hour half-life this is not a convenience: a page that re-ran the product on every build would print whatever the estate said today, and today's answer is guaranteed to differ from the pinned one, because this rung's freshest input decays faster than the gap between two sittings.

Every value in the snapshot sentence is verified against the tree at build time and from a source that did not come from the generator: the anchor and the instant against artifacts/question.yaml, the TTL against artifacts/ttl.yaml, the tolerance against ccf_config.yaml and the refused transcript, the three headline figures against the served transcript and against artifacts/expected.yaml — which was measured directly against the estate before a line of this product's code existed, and so is the only source here that cannot have been produced by the program the page is about.

Reproduce with make -s example-cp-05 after exporting the four connection variables the README describes. The claim is same question → same answer against a quiescent estate for that run, and no stronger claim is made anywhere: a reader whose figures differ has re-derived, not reproduced, and the most likely reason is that a balance moved — a property of the estate rather than of the product.

A threshold moves the projection; it never moves the truth

The Detailed Consumer drops the materiality floor from 506,250,000 to 202,500,000 — and the projection does not move. This family's book is concentrated in five entities and the smallest material one commits 800,000,000, so both floors select the same four. The measurement is worth more than the movement would have been: the family, the gross and the adjusted totals are byte-identical to the default consumer's, which is the invariant. A threshold that changed the totals would be a request parameter altering truth. Only the content address differs, because the question differed.

The Material-Only Consumer is the threshold that does move this answer: at a 1,012,500,000 floor the 800,000,000 obligor falls into the residual — 3 enumerated instead of 4, residual 945,000,000 instead of 145,000,000 — and the same family and the same totals again. Zero-exposure members reached only by guarantee or cross-default are never absorbed into a residual at any threshold; absorbing them would delete the finding the traversal exists to produce.

Two maximum constraints, two behaviors

The Regulated Consumer asks for 1.0 under a regime that will not permit it, and policy narrows the bar to 0.10; the product is then withheld. The Appetite Consumer asks for a coarser materiality than its regime allows, and the request is refused by name rather than clamped.

The same operator, two behaviors, and the difference is the whole of it: materiality shapes the answer, debt tolerance judges it. A narrowed request answers a question nobody asked, so a shaping parameter outside its bound is refused. A fitness bar, by contrast, cannot be refused before fitness has been measured — so policy binds the bar and the comparison happens after assembly, where the observation exists. This is the SDK's one narrowing case and it is deliberate.

The As-Of Consumer is refused for a third reason again: this product's TTL is anchored from_decision, so there is no entity validity period an as_of could address, and the ledger's transaction counter is transaction time which D-IMPL-88 rules must not back it. Declared and refused, never silently ignored — an as_of quietly dropped returns a present-tense answer wearing a past-tense label.

The code, and what stopped being in it

The seam is unchanged from the earlier rungs: deployment.py names every query, table, column, predicate and domain word and makes no fabric call; produce.py makes every fabric call and names no table, no column and no domain word. Adapting this example to another estate touches the first and not the second.

What changed at this rung is the other axis. produce_governed used to compose the governed spine by hand — gates, contract construction, debt placement, the tolerance comparison, refusal shaping, the replay write and the serve. Adopting the canonical GovernedResolver path removed all of it. The function is now an adapter into the SDK rather than a smaller second implementation of it: it loads declarations, acquires content, shapes the business payload, and hands the resolver its extension points.

The MCP door, measured

server/serve.py exposes the governed path as an authenticated, entitled MCP tool that records every denial as a decision. It re-implements no part of the assembly, the gates, the debt assessment, the tolerance comparison or the replay record. Measured after the migration: 204 executable lines, zero of them governance or replay logic. Before the migration that measurement would have been meaningless, because the door wrapped a hand-composed spine and “MCP cost” and “governance cost” were the same number.

The door got thinner, not fatter. It used to run a full conformity check of its own; that was removed, because a door owns transport, address and standing, while the resolver owns contract-aware decoding, conformity, effective resolution and the governed outcome. The door is intentionally dumb about logical parameter types: it transports wire-stable lexical values, and the resolver — which holds the declaration — converts and judges them.

One thing the door was built to measure rather than to fix: it proves a record exists, and it does not read it. A served product whose payload claimed 9,999 family members, wrapping a replay record whose first dimension observed {{family}}, would cross the boundary unremarked. Both operands are in the door's hand and the comparison is not made. Recorded, not closed.

Scope

What this example is not

It is not a credit opinion. The anchor is real — a real identifier, a real US-MN jurisdiction, a real SIC code — and the bank's book against it is engineered. No bank lends to this obligor on these terms, because the lender does not exist. The topology was built so both halves of the failure are demonstrable, and calibrated so committed exposure is 49.4% of the obligor's reported total debt, a plausible share the estate itself flags.

It is not a materialized product, and that is a design refusal rather than an omission. The fastest input has a four-hour half-life, so a materialized rollup would be stale inside its own window. Rung 4 uses the same traversal machinery and materializes; this one must not.

It is not a complete group. One anchor was chosen because one anchor exercises everything this rung teaches: a real, small own-book that the guarantee adjustment cannot hide inside rounding; a deductible guarantee 5.5× the size of that book; four entities reached that hold nothing at all; and a cross-default reach outside the ownership group entirely.

Findings raised, and how they were handled

The migration onto the canonical path was run under a standing rule: if the canonical path exposes a new SDK deficiency, stop at the evidence, classify it, and obtain a ruling before any SDK repair. Five were raised that way and each was ruled rather than worked around — among them a binder that had to move to the canonical seam, decimal values arriving over a wire whose stable types are string, integer and boolean, and a door that was preempting a conformity check the resolver owns.

One is preserved deliberately as a record rather than a defect. artifacts/replay.yaml exists because the Control Plane document's section vocabulary was closed and refused a replay: section by name. The refusal was correct — a Control Plane that accepted arbitrary sections would let a deployment smuggle unaudited state into the audit plane — and the SDK has since added the section, so the binding is now declared in ccf_config.yaml directly. The document remains as the record of the finding.

And one behavior was recorded rather than repaired: the anchor falling below its own materiality floor. An example surfaces this; it does not quietly special-case the anchor to make the output look friendlier.
