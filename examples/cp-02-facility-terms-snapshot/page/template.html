Orientation

The rung where the anchor starts to cost something

Rung 01 introduced ttl_anchor: from_entity_freshness against a vendor file that republishes daily, so the expiry and the load date were never far apart. This product anchors on a credit agreement, and those are signed years apart. The choice becomes visible, and getting it wrong becomes expensive.

It is also the first product on the ladder that anchors on something that is not a legal entity. A facility is an instrument: its identifier is internal, no GLEIF stands behind it, and an anchor that does not resolve cannot be distinguished from one that never existed. That is why a miss here is a 404 and never an empty payload — an empty payload for an instrument reads as “this facility has no terms.”

What the failure actually is

A loan-system screen returns every term a credit officer needs. What it cannot return is a statement of when believing it stops being safe. Terms do not decay on a clock — they are valid until the agreement is amended — so an expiry measured from the moment somebody asked resets every time anybody asks. A staleness control that resets on being read is not a control.

The two lines that are the product

ttl basis names which date the expiry was measured from, so a consumer can check the arithmetic without reading the specification. Without it, two products anchored on different dates are indistinguishable on the wire.

And expires at does not move. Ask the same facility seventeen days later and it reads 2026-10-27 again — because it is the agreement's date plus ninety days, and the question is not an input to that sum.

The uncomfortable half

At that instant, 50 of the 60 facilities in this estate refuse on ttl-bounds — and all 43 that carry an amendment history are among them. The estate holds an amendment count and no amendment date, so nothing can establish that the terms on file are the terms in force.

That is recorded as a finding and returned to the vault, not smoothed away. The alternative that would make every facility serve — anchoring on the extraction date instead — turns out to carry one distinct value across all 60 rows, which is a product-level TTL wearing a per-entity label. The number that settles it is 1, not 60.

Refusals

Four ways this product declines, and each is correct

Terms older than the window

Eleven amendments, an agreement signed outside the ninety-day window, and the TTL gate refuses. This is 50 of 60 facilities today.

A question you may not ask

A principal granted every jurisdiction in the estate and no facility-level need-to-know is refused — with the same sentence, to the byte, that a facility which does not exist returns. The natural message, “you are not entitled to see this facility,” is a disclosure: it confirms the facility exists. Someone could walk a list of identifiers and recover the client list one refusal at a time.

The distinction is not lost. It moves to the replay record, which holds exactly which principal asked and what was withheld. The audit knows; the caller does not.

Arithmetic that cannot be true

Four facilities carry a maturity date 196 days before their effective date — all four extracted from a single filing. The product serves both dates exactly as filed and marks the derived tenor NOT_EVALUABLE. A report would have shown −196 days, or filed the facility into the shortest bucket of a maturity ladder.

A parameter this tier declares and always refuses

as_of returns 400 not_supported_at_tier. It matters more here than at rung 01: a facility carrying twenty-one amendments visibly has a history to ask about, so this is a question a caller will actually ask.

Boundaries

What this example is not

No history — reconstructing terms as they stood on a past date needs ledger time-travel and arrives at covenant-in-force-as-of. No pointer into the source agreement text, although the character offsets are populated on 60 of 60 rows and sit in the same row as the terms; a Tier-1 example that reaches into a document has silently become a Tier-4 one, and a gate asserts that none of them reaches the payload. No covenants, no collateral, no exposure rollup, no syndicate detail.

And no calibration. The four decay rates are expert judgment, not measurement, and the specification says so on every row — values an engineer can challenge rather than numbers anyone should assume were derived.
