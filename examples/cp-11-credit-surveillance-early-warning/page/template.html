Rung 11 · Tier 3

A fact that never becomes false, but can stop being relevant

An 8-K acceleration filing is as true a year later as the day it landed. signal_weight.decay_days is NULL for that whole class, and it is right to be. What ends such a filing's relevance is not time passing — it is a later filing.

Read in the obvious direction, “does not decay” becomes “counts forever”, and the 220 routine material-agreement notices and 184 officer changes drown the four accelerations that matter. Measured: 30 of 50 obligors breach the highest escalation band and 48 of 50 breach the lowest. A screen that fires on the whole book is not a screen.

Read correctly — supersession ends it — the same machinery, the same weights and the same curves put three names at the top band. Nothing changed but the set.

Three states, and they partition

Absence is not supersession

Every signal carries exactly one state: current, superseded, or not-participating. The third is its own answer and never collapses into the second — a signal the chain does not mirror has not been superseded, because it was never in a chain to be superseded from.

Two raw sets that overlap, and one classifier that cannot

Fluree gives back chained and current, and current is a subset of chained — so anything adding them counts every current signal twice. The product exposes one three-valued classifier and the raw sets never reach the payload. On the headline serve the counts are 1 current, 25 superseded, 113 not-participating, and they sum to 139: the number of signals in the delta, exactly.

And which classes participate is DECLARED, never inferred. Today only 8k_event has a chain. That is what this snapshot contains, not a law — and a product that read participation off the data would change behavior the day another class gained one, in the most dangerous direction: by silently beginning to drop signals nobody asked it to drop.

The cross-product case

An event about one entity makes a different product ineligible

This is what the rung exists for, and what D-IMPL-334 was built to make possible. An acceleration lands; the obligor's context moves; the fabric records an invalidation boundary at the instant the evidence became true in the world.

boundary  2026-07-28    (NOT 2026-08-19, when the fabric wrote it)

A1  read that obligor at an older snapshot   REFUSED
B1  did NOT, built at the SAME instant       SERVES
A2  derived after the boundary               SERVES

The middle row is the one that matters. B is exactly as old as A. A mechanism keyed on elapsed time rather than on what a product actually read would refuse it too — it would satisfy the first row and be a global clock wearing an invalidation's name.

And the refused product need never have mentioned early warning. The covenant product does not know this product exists. It is refused because it read an entity whose context moved. That is the cascade in the only sense the SDK can honestly claim.

What does NOT happen, and the gate that keeps it that way

Nobody is notified. Nothing is recalled. Nothing is evicted — there is no product cache to evict from. The fabric refuses to re-serve an obsolete answer; it cannot reach a copy a consumer already holds, and no system can. EWS-G22 asserts the payload never claims otherwise, over the payload and over every shipped source file.

Two SDK rulings, and the second was a defect in the first

The example is a probe, and this is the clearest instance yet

D-IMPL-334 built serve-time dependency invalidation after Gate 1 was held rather than shipping a substitute. It shipped with 14 tests and 8 sabotages, all green.

And it recorded the wrong instant. The writer took claim.observed_at — which, through L2 assembly, is always the ingestion instant, because D-IMPL-332 fixed observed_at as when the fabric learned and valid_from as when it became true. A world-time snapshot was being compared against a fabric-learning-time boundary.

Not merely conservative

It refuses products whose snapshot falls after the world change but before ingestion — on the basis of when the fabric happened to learn something, rather than on when anything changed. A refusal keyed to ingestion latency is keyed to an accident of operations, and the interval it wrongly covers is exactly as wide as the lag between the world and the fabric.

No test in tests/ could see it. Every one built its claims directly, where observed_at is whatever the author supplied and the two clocks never separate. Only a Context Product going through real assembly could. D-IMPL-335 corrected it, and its regression gives two envelopes the same ingestion_timestamp and different observed_time — so ingestion cannot be what distinguishes them.

The evidence

Twenty-nine gates, and three of them were wrong first

Every gate was sabotaged. Twenty-five went red on their own assertion. Three could not be falsified from this deployment at all — an SDK guard refuses the bad state before an artifact can exist — and are recorded unavailable, never folded into passed. One has nothing to range over on this estate and skips, because a green assertion over an empty set is not evidence.

A gate asserted only where two answers coincide proves nothing about which one produced them

EWS-G27 stayed green under a sabotage that inferred supersession participation from the data — because on this estate the inferred set and the declared set are the same value. EWS-G29 stayed green under a sabotage crossing the two decay models, because structure and correlation both survived it; only recomputing the arithmetic caught it. And EWS-G20 went red against correct code, by searching source text and tripping on the comment that explains its own rule.

All three are recorded in artifacts/gates.yaml with what was wrong and what fixed it. Deleting that history would hide the strongest thing this roster knows about itself.

What to change first

One line, and it is the most instructive edit in the tree

  • supersession_participants in artifacts/source-vocabulary.yaml. Today it names 8k_event alone. Empty it and every signal becomes not-participating — nothing is dropped, and the composite climbs to the accumulating reading that puts 30 of 50 obligors at the board band. The rung's central trap, reachable in one edit.
  • The three floors in artifacts/escalation-policy.yaml, to watch the band populations move. The estate's own values are carried beside them in the payload, so the difference stays visible.
  • --since, to move the delta's left edge and see what changes scope without changing how anything is scored.

Honest limits

What this example is not

  • No delta between two ledger points. The catalog asks for one. Measured, this estate's entire signal population arrives in a single bulk load between t=421 and t=491 and nothing is ever added, so a ledger delta returns everything or nothing. The product uses valid time and declares the substitution rather than making it quietly.
  • No per-mention media sentiment. tone looks like a score and is a query filter — all 951 rows carry the identical string <-5. Weighting by it would weight every mention by a constant while appearing to discriminate.
  • No Item 4.02 non-reliance events. Two obligors carry a lifetime count; none falls in the window. The auditor leg is Item 4.01 only.
  • One cold-start anchor per obligor, all on the same date. The estate seeds a starting point, not a sweep history, so the default delta is the full window for every name.
  • 32 of 36 WARN name matches are wrong — TransDigm to Transdev, B&G Foods to US Foods, SP Plus to Lamps Plus, at score ≈0.71. Only the four resolved matches contribute, and the withheld count is published with its reason rather than dropped in silence.

Illustrative calibration. This example runs against a point-in-time demonstration estate rather than continuously refreshed production data.
