Orientation

Where one freshness per product stops being enough

Rungs 01 and 02 each carry one expiry for the whole answer, and that is honest because each assembles a document from a single act of reporting. A spread cannot. It is stitched from many filings, and a payload holding last week's quarter beside a four-year-old annual is not uniformly anything.

So temporal validity moves down a level. Every figure carries its own period_end, filed_date and accession; two lines in one column routinely name two different filings. The product's own expiry is anchored on the latest filing in the payload — never on the date the question was asked, because an expiry that tracked the question would make every product permanently fresh.

What the failure actually is

A spreading tool returns every figure a credit officer needs. What it cannot return is which of those figures the obligor has since corrected. The numbers arrive either way; only one version is the one the company now stands behind. Two of the sixteen columns in the run below were restated, and nothing in a plain extract would have said so.

The same primitive, opposite behavior

This rung and the one before it anchor their expiry on the same field type and behave in opposite ways — which is what makes from_entity_freshness teachable rather than merely declared.

ProductAnchored onIts refusals
cp-02a signed agreement date50 of 60, permanently — the date can never advance
cp-03a filing date27 of 50, and they clear as filers file

The difference is entirely whether the anchored field is re-observed. Neither rung teaches that alone.

And the first parameter where both settings are correct

basis selects which filing's value wins. as-reported is what the obligor said at the time; latest-restated is what it says now. Neither is the truth — the decision picks. For an obligor carrying a restatement the two produce different content addresses, so a cache can never hand one back for the other.

Refusals

Three ways this product declines, and each is correct

An as-of date is refused, never honored quietly. Reconstructing the spread as it stood on a past date is a Tier 3 capability. The dangerous outcome is not the refusal — it is accepting the parameter and ignoring it, which answers a question about today while the caller believes they asked about last March.

An obligor outside the caller's entitlement is indistinguishable from one that does not exist. A refusal reading “you are not permitted to see this” would confirm to an unentitled caller that the obligor is real. In a bank with information barriers that is the leak, not the protection.

An obligor whose newest filing is older than the window is refused on TTL bounds — 27 of the 50 at this pin. Those clear on their own as the obligor files again, which is exactly what rung 02's permanent refusals cannot do.

Fourteen gates, and every one made to fail

A gate that has never been observed red is not evidence that it runs. Each of the fourteen was sabotaged and the observed result recorded in the specification's §G.2.

Three passed their first sabotage, all for the same reason, and the finding outlasts the gates: one counted the declared kind of a line the obligor never filed as evidence of a real fact; one matched a word sitting in its own docstring; one watched a seal that always changes because the payload records which basis it was read under.

A check that reads a token present in both the compliant and the violating case is measuring vocabulary, not content.

Scope

What this example is not

It is not a credit opinion. The estate is public filing data used to exercise mechanism, and nothing here is a view on any company.

It is not a complete spread on its anchor. The demonstration obligor was chosen for restatement, not for completeness — it files no depreciation, so four ratio lines are permanently unavailable, and it has no peer cohort. Two documented variations supply what it cannot, because no obligor in this estate carries everything.

And one question was raised here and then settled. The specification required identical content addresses for an unrestated obligor read under both bases, and that does not hold as built. The first account of it — “the payload merely echoes which basis it was read under” — was incomplete: the two readings genuinely consult different filings for 20 of 75 figures and report the same number from each. No served figure moves, and the documents still differ, in provenance.

Making the seals match would have meant suppressing which filing a figure came from wherever the number happened to agree — deleting a true fact to make a hash match, at the rung whose subject is precisely that. The criterion was amended instead (D-IMPL-222): for an unrestated obligor the two bases must change no figure, which is what the gate asserts.
