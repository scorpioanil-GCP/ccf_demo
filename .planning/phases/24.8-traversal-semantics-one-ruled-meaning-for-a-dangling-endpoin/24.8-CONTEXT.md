## Phase Boundary

One SDK defect, nothing else: **G-28** — two implementations of one traversal semantics disagree on a graph containing a dangling edge (an edge claim whose endpoint has no entity claim). Dave's framing, verbatim: *"Before changing code, determine what a dangling endpoint means in CCF traversal semantics, then require both implementations to produce the same governed result."*

Today (measured, `24.8-RULINGS.md` §2): the query tier (`src/ccf/graph/fallback.py::answer` → `_endpoint_closure` → `_walk`, and the five pushed adapters held to it differentially) endpoint-closes edges against the surviving entity universe BEFORE walking, so a claim-less endpoint is never a hop. The operator tier (`src/ccf/operators/categories.py::_bounded_walk`, run by `TraversalNeighborhood` and `TraversalDescendants` over the store's as-of view, which keeps dangling edges as state) does not endpoint-close first: with `admitted_entity_classes=None` it steps onto the claim-less URI, spends depth on it, and reaches the claimed nodes beyond it. `docs/GRAPH_QUERY_SEMANTICS.md` 1.2.0 is silent on the operator tier's walk. No test covers the shape.

After this phase: ONE traversal-family rule, written into the semantics document and binding every implementation — *construct the admitted entity universe first; endpoint-close edges against that universe; then walk* — with the in-process operator conforming and a new differential holding operator-tier results to the query-tier/reference semantics, byte-identical under canonical rendering.

## Implementation Decisions

All eight ruled by Dave at this sitting, 2026-09-06 11:29 MST, recorded at `docs/DECISIONS.md` `D-IMPL-267` and VERBATIM in `24.8-RULINGS.md` §3 (D-NN here = Q-NN there; the verbatim file governs). Two principles (P-1, P-2) are his framing of the whole.

### What a dangling endpoint is
- **D-01 (Q1 b):** an **unresolved reference** — legal persisted graph state (edges are first-class claims, INV-CH7-20; the everything-answer keeps them, `GQ-05`/`GQ-21`), NOT a traversal member until an entity claim exists for it. *"an edge assertion [does not create] an entity assertion."*
- **D-02 (Q2 a):** traversal membership REQUIRES an entity claim. The invariant, his words: *"Edges may reference unresolved entities, but traversal operates over claimed entities."*
- **D-03 (Q3):** NOT admitted; NO placeholder. No synthetic entity record, no provenance-marked stand-in — *"That would be worse than the current divergence."*

### What exclusion is
- **D-04 (Q4 a, with a carry):** exclusion is a **traversal semantic**, not a finding and not an invariant failure: *"Traversal silently excludes the unresolved endpoint from path formation."* Whether the existence/count of excluded unresolved references later becomes execution/completeness evidence is **explicitly deferred** — carry `24.8-C-01`, not a 24.8 requirement, and *"I do not want fixing traversal equivalence to grow into product findings/debt design."*

### Equivalence
- **D-05 (Q5 b, defined):** **byte-identical canonical traversal results** — first the semantic result identical (same admitted entities, same admitted edges, same depth assignments/path consequences), then the normal canonical ordering/rendering identical bytes. NOT identical internal collections, query plans, or backend response ordering. The invariant: *"Equivalent traversal questions over equivalent pinned state must yield the same canonical traversal result regardless of execution path."*

### Anchors, depth, cycles
- **D-06 (Q6 a):** one rule — a claim-less URI is neither a valid walk start nor an intermediate hop; one ontology of "entity" for anchors and neighbors.
- **D-07 (Q7 a):** an excluded endpoint consumes NO depth and participates in NO path or cycle: *"Depth counts admitted traversal hops, not persisted edge hops through entities that do not exist in the traversal universe."* Two implementations agreeing on the entity set but disagreeing on depth is a defect.

### Where the rule lives
- **D-08 (Q8 a):** generic traversal-family semantics, NOT an adapter capability (*"A capability flag would institutionalize the divergence"*). The query-tier rule becomes the family rule: **construct the admitted entity universe first; endpoint-close edges against that universe; then walk.** The in-process operator conforms to that sequence; the new differential compares operator-tier results against the query-tier/reference semantics.

### The two principles (Dave, verbatim)
- **P-1:** *"Persisted graph state and traversal membership are not the same universe. An edge may legally name an unresolved endpoint; that does not make the endpoint traversable."*
- **P-2:** *"Traversal semantics are execution-path invariant. In-process, fallback, and pushed implementations may execute differently, but the same question over the same pinned state must produce the same canonical traversal result."*

### Claude's Discretion
- The amendment's placement and version in `docs/GRAPH_QUERY_SEMANTICS.md` (a `GQ-10`/`GQ-13`/`GQ-16` amendment under `GQ-35`'s history; presumptively 1.3.0), in the form the 1.0.1/1.1.0/1.2.0 amendments took.
- The mechanism by which `_bounded_walk` conforms (endpoint-close the traversable edges against the view's entity universe before building adjacency, or restrict adjacency to claimed URIs — one function, as today, so the two operators cannot drift).
- The shape and home of the new operator-tier-vs-query-tier differential (beside `tests/conformance/test_substrate_differential.py`, reusing `query_corpus.py`'s dangling and orphan cases), and the shared canonical rendering it compares (the `ccf.contracts.canonical` bytes are the candidate).
- The measurable form of D-07 (per-node depth is not emitted today; depth-limited reach sets with and without the unresolved endpoint on the path are the observable).
