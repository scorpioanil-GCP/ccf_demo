### WARNING-1 — the narrowing is honest at the goal, weakest at the scope preamble

**Not a blocker. Needs a ruling.** Every *enumerated* claim in the narrowed ROADMAP text is
literally TRUE and was verified above: no new transport, no new adapter, no `mcp+http`, no
change to the client seam. `D-IMPL-153` is accurate to the shipped code down to the
`OPTIONAL_KEYS`-not-`RULED_OUT_KEYS` distinction. The surface fragment records **all four**
public-surface changes with full Before/After/Why/Migration, so no adopter is misled.

The one sentence that reads thin is the scope preamble, unchanged in substance by the
narrowing:

> **Scope — three changes, none of them new mechanism, plus the one refusal `D-18` rules in**

Change #1 in fact added, in `src/`: a new public field on `BindingDocument`, a new member of
the public `OPTIONAL_KEYS` register, a new containment base for one key, and **three** new
named refusal branches (`D-06`, `D-07`, `D-08`) that refuse an address which parsed before.
Under the phase's own working definition — *mechanism* = a new enforcement rule beyond the
announced scope, with the grammar flip counting as a *replacement* of an existing token-count
refusal rather than an addition — the sentence is defensible. Under the plainer reading it
undercounts. Both readings are consistent with the evidence; the choice is Dave's, and it is
listed as human verification item 1.

I record it as a warning rather than a blocker because the complete and accurate record
exists one document away, and because the fragment's own summary sentence — *"Everything
else the phase changed is example prose, example layout and test machinery"* — parses
correctly when read against its antecedent (*"this fragment is the record"*), i.e. *everything
not recorded here*. Under that reading the fragment is complete and true.
