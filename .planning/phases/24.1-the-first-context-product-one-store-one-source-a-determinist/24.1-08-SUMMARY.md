## Two instrument defects found in the guard machinery, both in-session

**Wave 8 is the tenth consecutive wave to find its own defect class inside its
own machinery, and this wave's is the same class: the mechanism was right and
the proof machinery was wrong.**

**Defect 1 — a blockquote marker made two present statements read as absent.**
The first draft wrapped statements (ii) and (iv-a) across source lines inside a
Markdown blockquote. Whitespace-flattening (`re.sub(r'\s+', ' ', text)` — the
plan's own predicate) collapses the newline but leaves the continuation line's
`>` marker sitting inside the sentence, so `…names and > relationships` does not
match `names and relationships`. The guard reported both absent when both were
present. **Remedy: every D-35 statement is now written on one source line.** This
is exactly the instrument error the plan's own comment warned about — *"a line
break inside the sentence cannot make a present statement read as absent"* — and
the plan's flattening does not in fact prevent it inside a blockquote.

**Defect 2 — one control took down a statement it did not own.** Statements
(iv-a) and (iv-b) were first written as one sentence joined by a semicolon. The
control for (iv-a) cuts from the preceding period to the following period, which
removed (iv-b) as well: the control fired, but for two statements rather than
one, so (iv-b) had no control of its own. Caught only because the guard asserts
control **isolation** and not merely control firing. **Remedy: the two limbs are
two sentences.** A control that also takes down its neighbour is red for the
wrong reason, which is Wave 7's lesson arriving in a different shape.

**Defect 3 — a threat-model row was written against a pattern that could not
have produced the number beside it.** The first draft of the `T-24.1-25` row
said the octet/credential sweep *"returns exactly one line: the README's own
sentence."* It does not: the sentence contains `.env`, and `.env` was not in
that pattern. The one-line reading came from a *different*, earlier sweep that
included `\.env`, and the two got conflated while writing the row up. Caught by
re-running the pattern as written rather than trusting the recollection of what
it returned. Corrected to two sweeps with their two counts and a positive
control. Recorded because it is the project's recurring counting error in its
purest form — **a number reported next to an instrument that did not produce
it** — and it reached a draft of a deliverable before it was caught.
