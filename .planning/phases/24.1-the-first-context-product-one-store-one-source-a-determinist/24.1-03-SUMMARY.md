## THE FINDING THAT IS DAVE'S: `D-39`'s layer, measured

`D-39` (Dave, 2026-08-26): *"Use an **L2** Resolve from (lei, period) to the
dominant CUSIP."*

**Measured:** `src/ccf/assembly/worker.py::EntityResolutionStrategy.resolve`
takes `(*, envelope)` — **one envelope at a time** — and
`src/ccf/assembly/worker.py::AssemblyWorker` drives it per envelope. A
*dominant* CUSIP is a group-and-rank over many rows. A strategy computing it
would have to accumulate state across envelopes and become order-dependent,
which is exactly the determinism §D.4's replay assertion rests on.

**So the resolve is two steps at two layers**: the group-and-rank at the source
read (**L1**, the capability §C.2 already listed as the one most likely to be
partial), and the canonical-entity resolution at **L2** exactly as the mapping
hypothesis predicts.

**Every reason in `D-40` still holds and nothing in the ruling's substance
changes.** No literal CUSIP is pinned. No domain value enters code. The
fan-out is handled rather than hidden. The `Resolve` name is placed at a layer,
which is what criterion 11 asks for — it is placed at *two*. **What differs
from the ruling's words is the layer of one half**, and it is written into
§D.3 rather than absorbed. **Dave's to confirm or correct.**

## AN INSTRUMENT OF MY OWN THAT REPORTED A MISMATCH THAT DID NOT EXIST

My first criteria-block extractor produced sha256 `c19fc4c4…a3487` against
Wave 1's recorded `15d3ef66…c9300f`. **Read at face value that is a criteria
block that changed — a `D-GATE-19` violation and a hard stop.**

It changed nothing. The two extractors differ in whitespace handling: Wave 1
joined the **non-blank** lines and appended a newline; mine took the raw slice.
Re-run under Wave 1's normalization the sha reproduces **exactly**, from two
different start anchors.

**A sha256 over a hand-bounded string is only comparable under an identical
extractor**, and reporting a "mismatch" would have been an instrument error
presented as a finding — the class this project has now been bitten by
repeatedly. The superseding instrument is the one that needs no extractor
at all: `git diff phase-24.1-base -- .planning/ROADMAP.md` is **empty**, so
the criteria are byte-identical to the base by construction.

independently prove ran**, which is exactly criterion 2's RED condition.

### Task 2 — §E, §F, §G, and `D-43`

**§E.** λ_c **137 days**, basis **illustrative**, decay driver the quarterly
filing cycle, supersession event *an amendment restating the same period* —
not the arrival of the next quarter. The anchor is `report_period`, up to 90
days coarse, and the coarseness is a property of 13F rather than a data defect
(`D-22`).

**F over rows ASSEMBLED, not SELECTED** — `FreshnessScorer` refuses an empty

*raise* instead of answering. **Decay from `valid_from`, not `observed_at`** —

scoring it measures the age of *this run*.

**C splits into `C_attributes × C_match`**, because this product has two
different incompletenesses and one number would hide both. And the question
the plan told me to answer either way: **the 115 weak rows are a completeness
shortfall this product DECLARES, not a scope boundary it draws** — the reason
being that the rows are **served**, and a product that includes them owes the
consumer a number saying how much of the answer rests on a weaker match.

**S is 1 with the reason stated**: one source cannot disagree with itself.
