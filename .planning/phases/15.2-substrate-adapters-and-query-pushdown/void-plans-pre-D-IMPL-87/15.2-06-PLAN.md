<objective>
Make operators declare pushability (D-03) and make the engine own the split
(D-01): read_intent() on the Operator surface, a pure segmentation function
implementing the prose's first-maximal-pushable-prefix rule, the engine's
execute loop reworked to issue reader.query() per segment, and the executed
query-hash sequence pinned into replay dimension 5 following the
workflow_step_sequence sentinel precedent.

Review-decided remedy for the double-application question (Consensus concern
2): the engine re-runs pushed operators over the returned view (double
application), and the soundness of that shape rests on the prose's
idempotence-over-pushed-views GQ clause written in plan 15.2-01 — a proof in
semantics, not an engine-local assumption. The alternative (recording pushed
operators as executed without re-applying them, with provenance minted from the
planned operator + query pin) was considered and rejected because it violates
the repo's established "the engine records what ran, never what was declared"
pattern: it would mint provenance from plan-side prediction. Double application
keeps every provenance fact execution-derived. The price is that Gate 2 must be
PINNED, not presumed: the end-to-end regression asserts lineage strict
equality (operators_invoked, provenance/parameter hashes, run_gates →
ServableProduct) for BOTH plan shapes, not only product canonical_bytes.

Hard constraints: execute()'s signature and plan.required_operators() stay
byte-stable (the 15.1 binder sits on those seams); the stage order (operators →
acquisition → Π) survives; the engine records what ran, never what was declared.

Output: read_intent across the four shipped operators, segmentation, the replay
pin, and the equivalence + matrix + Gate-2 regression tests.
</objective>
