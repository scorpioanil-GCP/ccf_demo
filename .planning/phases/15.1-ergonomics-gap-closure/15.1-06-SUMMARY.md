## 2. The three rewrites

Each site got the identical shape, and the three new documents are the only place their operator
sets are authored a second time:

```python
declared = cast(ProductClassDeclaration, booted.artifacts["product-class"])
requirements = cast(ProjectionRequirements, booted.artifacts["projection-requirements"])
...
plan = <ExamplePlan>(plan_id=requirements.plan_id, required=requirements.required_operator_keys)
refuse_operator_set_divergence(declared=declared, plan=plan)

...
lineage=ProductLineage(operators_invoked=frozenset(declared.required_operator_keys), ...)

```

**The hoist was load-bearing at all three sites, exactly as 15.1-04 predicted for the quickstart.**
Every one of the three constructed its plan *inline as the `plan=` keyword argument* of the
`.execute(...)` call, so the interval the corrected D-IMPL-86 placement names was empty — there was
no statement between "the plan exists" and "the engine runs it" because the plan existed only for
the duration of one call expression.

of what the fabric was permitted to do, so it belongs in replay dimension 2 beside the product
class whose claim the binder holds it to.

## Deviations from Plan

### Findings recorded rather than fixed

**1. [Finding] The `operators_invoked=execution.operators_invoked` grep-0 criterion is not
satisfiable jointly with the `engine_resolved=execution.operators_invoked` grep-1 criterion**

- **Found during:** Task 1, repeated identically at Tasks 2 and 3
- **Issue:** After the lineage rewrite, each of the three files retains **exactly one** occurrence
  of `operators_invoked=execution.operators_invoked`, and in every case it is a **result-carrier
  field**, not the lineage:
  - `examples/03-harness-portability/run.py:480` — `DecisionBundle.operators_invoked`
  - `examples/04-cross-process/producer_child.py:438` — `AssembledCovenantSlice.operators_invoked`
  - `examples/07-artifact-store-mcp/run.py:494` — `WorkloadResult.operators_invoked`

  Each carries the **engine's resolution** to the test module, where
  `test_lineage_strict_equality_holds` compares it against the product's claim. Filling it from
  `declared.required_operator_keys` instead would make that test compare a value against itself —
  precisely the collapse this phase exists to close.

  Every spelling that drives the grep to 0 breaks the other criterion:
  - naming the field `engine_resolved` → `grep -c "engine_resolved=execution.operators_invoked"`
    becomes **2**, and the criterion requires it to equal the number of `run_gates` call sites (1);
  - hoisting a local `engine_resolved = execution.operators_invoked` and passing it to both →
    that grep becomes **0**, and the criterion requires 1;
  - reformatting the expression across lines → grep-gaming, not a fix.

  `ServableProduct` exposes only `.product` (`src/ccf/products/gates.py:166-169`), so there is no
  third place the engine's resolution can be read from.
- **Resolution:** The plan's `<action>` text — "fill the lineage from
  `frozenset(declared.required_operator_keys)`; leave `engine_resolved` alone" — was applied
  exactly. The grep criterion is a proxy that over-reaches by one non-lineage site. **Not
  refactored**, deliberately: the same shape exists at all nine of CR-2's sites, plans 15.1-05 and
  15.1-07 are running concurrently against the same criterion, and a carrier renamed in three
  examples and not the other six would be worse than a uniformly recorded finding.
- **Routed to:** plan 15.1-09 (which owns the Gate 2 rationale text) and plan 15.1-10 (the gate),
  as a criterion-wording correction: the property is *the lineage is not filled from `execution`*,
  which is verifiable and true at all three sites.
