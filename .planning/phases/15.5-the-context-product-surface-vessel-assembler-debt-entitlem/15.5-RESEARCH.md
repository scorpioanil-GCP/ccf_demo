### Locked Decisions

**The vessel — LOCKED (`D-IMPL-97`)**

> *"The true ContextProduct is nothing more than a vessel that has been assembled to represent a unique capability for the calling agent to consume. Kind of like MCP — a standard ContextProduct doesn't really need to subclass, it just needs to have certain operations and payloads which we have already determined via the whitepaper."*

- **The payload is disciplined by a declared JSON Schema** registered with the product class, in the shape MCP tools declare `inputSchema`. Reuse the machinery already proven on eight artifact classes; do not invent a second one.
- **This is a wording correction to whitepaper §7.6, not a reversal** (Dave, explicitly: *"Vessel wording is not reversal"*). The intent — typed payload, cross-cutting invariants once on the base — is preserved.
- **Bound the MCP analogy.** MCP carries no governance semantics. The vessel is MCP-shaped in typing discipline *with a governance envelope MCP has no equivalent for*. Do not let a plan drift into "it's just MCP".

**The assembler — LOCKED (`D-IMPL-97`)**

> *"The assembler, which I agree should be a Protocol, is what makes the ContextProduct a KYC_ContextProduct."* … *"Agent becomes the implementation protocol."*

- **Protocol for the producer, vessel for the produced.**
- **The Protocol's implementation is a declared agent** from the `context_assembly` archetype — a Control Plane artifact from the Agent Factory, never SDK code and never a subclass.
- The assembler knows where its data lives and how to get it; talks to the six backends directly so the 15.2/15.3/15.4 adapter work is not lost; creates or uses tools; **may enlist other agents** to produce content, or stay deterministic; may keep its own KG in whichever of the six stores is simplest.
- The calling agent invokes the **product**, not an MCP server in front of a database. The orchestration mess is handled inside.
- **Products may be front-ended by MCP servers**, like databases are.
- **Products may aggregate other products.**

## Summary

The phase has one root cause and five symptoms. The root cause is that **`ContextProductContract` is an envelope describing an assembly that already happened, with no surface for the question that provoked it, no surface for how much to trust it, and no surface for who is entitled to receive it.** Five of the six axes of `G(E, R, T, P, Π, L)` reach the wire; the sixth signal — Context Debt, the one that says how much to trust the other five — does not, and the request that shaped the product is not on the wire at all. Everything Dave reacted to in `examples/s1-01` follows from that: with no request surface, the subject and the instant have nowhere to live but a YAML literal, and a product with a fixed subject at a fixed instant is by the whitepaper's own vocabulary *aggregated*, not *assembled*.

The good news, measured rather than hoped: **almost every mechanism this phase needs is already shipped somewhere else in the tree and simply is not wired here.** `SkillArgumentSchema` (`src/ccf/skills/catalog.py:209-250`) is already a typed, MCP-`inputSchema`-shaped declared schema with a lossless renderer and a closed-world `additionalProperties: false` — that is exactly D-IMPL-97's declared payload schema, and it needs no new dependency. `PeerTrustGrant` (`src/ccf/peering/trust_policy.py:97-169`) is per-peer entitlement over product classes with `extra="forbid"` and a field-enumeration test behind it — the local-principal form is the same shape on an easier boundary. `union_lineage` (`src/ccf/peering/admission.py:354-387`) is aggregate lineage composition, already property-tested. `QueryPinWriter` (`src/ccf/replay/serve.py:155-211`) already pins the literal question asked, and is unused by every example. `TenantAuthorizerProtocol` (`runtime/ccf_runtime/authz/protocol.py:77-91`) is a deny-by-default, per-call-re-resolved, both-outcomes-audited authorizer bound on the HITL server and the HTTP API but **not** on the MCP egress door. `DebtProfile` is already a *required* field on every agent definition (`docs/schemas/ccf.agent-definition.json` `required` includes `halflife_debt_profile`), so the assembler-as-agent already has a version-pinned home for its debt configuration.

The bad news, also measured: **one design decision genuinely cannot be deferred, and it is not the one the plan will want to start with.** `ReplayEnvironment.rehydrate_product` (`src/ccf/replay/engine.py:160-170`) rebuilds the delivered product from `(record, re-executed projection)` and the engine hash-checks the result against `content_generation_path.product_content_address`. Anything added to `ContextProductContract` that is not reconstructible from the recorded envelope plus the re-executed projection makes **every replay diverge**. Debt is time-varying by construction. So "where debt lands" (open question 3) is not a taste question; it is a structural precondition on the debt work, and it must be ruled before a line of it is planned.

**Primary recommendation:** plan this phase as **one gating decision wave, then four largely independent tracks, then a re-cut of `s1-01` that consumes all four.** The gating wave puts open questions 1, 3 and 7 to Dave as a `checkpoint:human-verify` with the options and consequences already written (they are in § Open Questions below, ready to paste). The four tracks — contract completeness + payload schema; debt delivery; entitlement at the door; assembler Protocol + aggregation — touch mostly disjoint modules and can run in parallel *after* the gate. The blast radius (§ Blast Radius) is large but almost entirely mechanical: ~30 files construct a `ContextProductContract` directly, and every one of them breaks the instant a required field is added, so the field-addition strategy (required vs. sentinel-defaulted) is itself a planning decision with a measured cost.

## The hard determinism constraint — read before planning any debt work

This is the single most load-bearing finding in this document, and the phase brief asked for it directly.

`ReplayEnvironment.rehydrate_product` (`src/ccf/replay/engine.py:160-170`):

> *"Rebuild the delivered product from the record and the re-execution. The payload comes from the re-executed projection; the envelope (schema, TTL anchor, expiry) comes from the deployment's product class. The engine checks the result's content address against the record, so an envelope reconstructed wrongly is caught rather than accepted."*

The engine then compares the rehydrated product's `content_hash` against `record.content_generation_path.product_content_address` and raises `ReplayDivergence` on mismatch (`src/ccf/replay/engine.py:446-452`). Replay divergence is **raised, not returned**, deliberately (`engine.py` docstring: *"a decision that does not reproduce is a finding"*).

Therefore, mechanically:

> **Any field added to `ContextProductContract` must be reconstructible at replay time from (a) the recorded replay record, (b) the deployment's registered product class, or (c) the re-executed projection's output. A field that is none of those three makes every replay of every product diverge.**

Debt is not (b) — it is not class governance. It is not (c) — `ProjectionOutput` carries `plan_id` and `payload` only (`src/ccf/projection/plan.py:75-86`), and debt is computed from observations and a `now` the projection never sees. So **if debt goes on the product, it must also go into the replay record**, and `rehydrate_product` must be able to read it back.

This is not an argument for one answer over another — it is the constraint that makes Q3 answerable:

- **"Product only"** is *not viable* without also recording it. It would ship a phase that breaks `tests/replay/test_engine_rehydration.py` and every example's replay leg.
- **"Record only"** is viable and cheap but fails criterion 1 ("every Context Product delivered to a consumer carries its Context Debt") and fails whitepaper `:2696` ("a runtime signal the agent must consume").
- **"Both"** is viable and is the only option satisfying both criterion 1 and replay. Cost: `rehydrate_product` gains a parameter or the record's debt dimension becomes an input to it, and the `ReplayEnvironment` Protocol (a public surface) changes.

There is a fourth option worth putting to Dave: **debt travels beside the product, not on it** — i.e. on `ServedProduct` (`src/ccf/replay/serve.py:72-82`), which already pairs the product with its `replay_ref` and is explicitly the thing *"a caller receives"*. `ServedProduct` is not content-hashed into the record, so nothing about replay changes; the consuming agent still receives debt on every delivery; and the delivered `ContextProductContract` bytes stay exactly what A2A signatures and content addresses already cover. The cost is that debt then does not cross the A2A/MCP-egress wire with the product (egress serializes `servable.product`, `runtime/ccf_runtime/mcp/egress.py:163`), which may or may not be acceptable — that is Dave's call.

I have not seen this fourth option in `CONTEXT_PRODUCT_REVISION.md` §9; it is a derivation from the tree, and it is offered as an option, not a recommendation.

### Pattern 1 — Class-level governance resolved at registration, refused loudly

**What:** governance a product class declares is resolved and validated at `register_product_class`, not at serve time.
**When:** freshness SLA, constituent-validity floors, payload schema, discovery/addressability declarations.
**Shipped precedent:**

```python
# src/ccf/products/contract.py:200-234 — abridged
def register_product_class(*, registry, schema_name, ..., max_ttl_key, max_ttl_version, ...):
    """... The MAX_TTL is resolved *here*, at registration, so a deployment that
    forgot to configure a ceiling finds out while wiring the fabric rather than
    when a product is about to be served — at which point the only remaining
    options are a silent default (forbidden) or a failure in front of a caller."""
    try:
        artifact = registry.resolve(max_ttl_key, max_ttl_version)
    except ControlPlaneMissing as missing:
        raise ProductClassRegistrationError(...) from missing
    if not isinstance(artifact, MaxTTL):
        raise ProductClassRegistrationError(...)
```

Note the pinned `(key, version)` pair rather than "latest", *"so that the ceiling a decision was gated against is re-resolvable years later"* — the same discipline applies to any new pinned governance artifact.

### Pattern 2 — Bind-time refusal across two separately authored artifacts

**What:** two Control Plane documents that must agree are compared before anything runs, and disagreement **refuses rather than repairs**.
**When:** criterion 8's freshness-SLA-exceeds-components refusal; D-IMPL-31's constituent-validity floors.
**Shipped precedent:**

```python
# src/ccf/deployment/binder.py:123-155 — abridged
def refuse_operator_set_divergence(*, declared: ProductClassDeclaration, plan: ProjectionPlan) -> None:
    """Raise if the product class's claim and the plan's requirements disagree.
    Call it between constructing the plan and calling execute().
    Raises ValueError: **Refused, never repaired** — the binder does not choose
    which document is right, because only the deployment's author knows which
    of the two they meant."""
```

Copy this posture exactly: refuse, name both sides, do not pick a winner.

### Pattern 3 — Entitlement as a typed Control Plane artifact whose *shape* forbids the bypass

**What:** entitlement is expressed as declared sets with no defaults, `extra="forbid"`, and the "cannot be turned off" claim is enforced by a **field-enumeration test**, not by prose.
**Shipped precedent:** `src/ccf/peering/trust_policy.py:97-169` and `tests/peering/test_trust_policy.py:213`:

```python
names = set(PeeringTrustPolicy.model_fields) | set(PeerTrustGrant.model_fields)
# asserts the union contains no field that could express a bypass
```

`PeerTrustGrant` docstring: *"Four declared sets, none with a default. Empty is expressible and means exactly what it says … because a grant that quietly defaulted to 'anything' would be the fast path this contract exists to make unreachable."* And: *"entitlement is checked after verification, never instead of it."*

**This is criterion 5's blueprint.** A `ProductEntitlementPolicy` keyed on tenant, mapping principal (or role, or scope) → `permitted_schema_names` + `permitted_roles`, with `grant_for()` raising `PrincipalNotEntitled` rather than returning a permissive empty grant, plus the field-enumeration test, reproduces every property on the local door.

**And it also gives criterion 4 its shape.** Perspective resolution at the door is *"which roles may this verified grant assert?"* — a `permitted_roles` set on the same grant, checked after authentication, before `Perspective` is constructed. `execute()` never learns any of this happened.

### Pattern 4 — Aggregate composition by set-union, already property-tested

```python
# src/ccf/peering/admission.py:354-387 — abridged
def union_lineage(*, producer: ProductLineage, consumer: ProductLineage) -> ProductLineage:
    return ProductLineage(
        operators_invoked=producer.operators_invoked | consumer.operators_invoked,
        policy_bundle_versions=producer.policy_bundle_versions | consumer.policy_bundle_versions,
        graph_snapshot_at=producer.graph_snapshot_at,   # single-valued; the producer's
    )
```

Reusable verbatim for criterion 8's lineage limb. Its property test lives at `tests/properties/test_lineage_union.py`. **Note the asymmetry:** `graph_snapshot_at` takes the producer's, and the docstring explains why order-independence is *"a law about the two set axes"* only. For an N-way composite of sibling components (not a producer/consumer pair), "whose `graph_snapshot_at`" is genuinely undefined by the current function — the TTL floor rule (`min(expires_at)`) has an obvious analogue (`min(graph_snapshot_at)`, the most conservative anchor) but **that is a design choice, not a derivation**, and the planner should say so rather than assume.

### Pattern 5 — Capability-token construction, for anything that must be unforgeable

Two shipped instances: `ServableProduct` (`src/ccf/products/gates.py:274-296`, module-private `_GATE_TOKEN`) and `DeliveryCapability` (`src/ccf/replay/serve.py:88-108`, `_DELIVERY_TOKEN`). Both raise a typed error on a wrong token. If criterion 1's *"no delivery path exists that drops it"* is to be structural rather than reviewed, this is the mechanism: make the thing that carries debt the only thing the delivery seam accepts.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- **Putting a clock in the engine.** CONTEXT.md `<specifics>` and `engine.py:415-419` both forbid it. The caller supplies `now`; a replay supplies the recorded one. The defect is the instant being a YAML literal, not the caller supplying it.
- **Deriving Perspective inside `execute()`.** Replay must feed the *recorded* perspective and must not re-authenticate a principal who may no longer exist. Door concern only.
- **Adding the entitlement check to `run_gates`.** The gates are pure over `(candidate, engine_resolved, now, registry, product_class)` and hold no principal. `run_federated_gates` shares `_refuse_empty_operators` and `_refuse_ttl` with the local path precisely so the two cannot drift; adding a principal to one and not the other reintroduces exactly that drift.
- **A `KYC_ContextProduct` class anywhere.** `D-IMPL-97`, and CLAUDE.md §6.1: an agent found in library code is a defect of the same severity as a hard-coded half-life table.
- **A weighted-additive debt form.** `src/ccf/halflife/debt.py:16-22` ships no weights and no additive path *"not as a default, at all"*, with the whitepaper's own worked example (0.955 multiplicative vs 0.23 additive on identical context) as the argument. Q6's composition weighting must not smuggle an additive form into the core.
- **A default entitlement grant.** Deny-by-default is the shipped posture on both the authorizer (`T-10-01`) and the trust policy. An "allow all product classes when unconfigured" default would be the fast path both exist to make unreachable.
- **Deleting `ubo-question.yaml` wholesale.** Only `subject_entity_ref` is the defect; the class/attribute vocabulary is correctly artifact-borne.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1 — Adding a required field to `ContextProductContract` and breaking ~30 files at once
**What goes wrong:** `CCFContract` is `extra=forbid` and frozen; a required field with no default breaks every one of the ~30 direct constructors in one commit, including two conformance modules and five perf suites.
**Why it happens:** the correct-looking design (debt is constitutive, so it is required) has the largest possible blast radius.
**How to avoid:** decide the field-addition strategy explicitly and early. The tree has a shipped precedent for the alternative — **positive sentinel defaults**: `PolicyBindingsSnapshot.hitl_actions = NO_HITL_ACTIONS`, `ModelVersionRecord.model_version = MODE_1_MODEL_VERSION`, `ContentGenerationPath.graph_query_sequence = NO_GRAPH_QUERIES`, `ContextFabricSnapshot.source_attestation = None` (`src/ccf/replay/record.py:281-284, 356-358, 446, 181`). Each is documented as a *positive assertion* ("this decision predates the seam") rather than an absence. That posture keeps every existing call site byte-for-byte unchanged and is what `run_gates`' additive attestation gate already does with `source_attestation: … = None`.
**Warning signs:** a plan whose verification step is "run the full suite and fix what breaks" rather than "N files enumerated, each with its owning task".

### Pitfall 2 — Making replay diverge on every product
**What goes wrong:** debt (or any time-varying field) lands on the product; `rehydrate_product` cannot reconstruct it; `ReplayDivergence` is *raised* on every replay.
**Why it happens:** the product feels like the natural home, and the rehydration seam is three modules away.
**How to avoid:** § "The hard determinism constraint". Resolve Q3 before planning the debt work; if the answer includes the product, the same plan must extend the record and `ReplayEnvironment.rehydrate_product` (a public Protocol → surface-changes fragment).
**Warning signs:** a debt plan with no task touching `src/ccf/replay/`.

### Pitfall 3 — Letting the entitlement check sink below the door
**What goes wrong:** the principal reaches `run_gates`, `serve()`, or the engine. Now the gates are impure, the federated and local gate sequences drift, and replay has to re-authenticate a principal who may no longer exist.
**Why it happens:** "check it where the product is made" is a natural instinct, and D-IMPL-68's ruling text already warns *"authorization never sinks below the door trust boundary"* for HITL.
**How to avoid:** the door decides; the engine receives an already-resolved `Perspective`. `tests/invariants/test_mcp_role_separation.py` is the shipped shape for asserting a seam holds structurally.
**Warning signs:** a `principal_id` parameter appearing anywhere in `src/ccf/products/` or `src/ccf/projection/`.

### Pitfall 4 — Encoding domain values while implementing "material event" / salience
**What goes wrong:** the core acquires a list of event types, a materiality threshold, or a salience vocabulary. `tools/lints/no_hardcoding.py` rule `cinv01b-numeric-table` catches the numeric form; **it does not catch a module-level tuple of strings**, so a domain vocabulary can slip through green.
**Why it happens:** "a material event transpired" is irreducibly domain-shaped, and Q4 is named in CONTEXT.md as *"the hardest line in the design"*.
**How to avoid:** the shipped answer for exactly this problem is `SkillRequirements` — *"Requirements are opaque identifiers (D-IMPL-29). The SDK never interprets an identifier: what any of them means is declared by the deployment's policy-regime artifacts, and no requirement vocabulary exists in this module"* (`src/ccf/skills/catalog.py:35-40`). Salience can be an opaque identifier set the deployment declares and the fabric only compares structurally.
**Warning signs:** any string literal in `src/` that a banker would recognize.

### Pitfall 5 — Reintroducing the "no fabric change" reflex under a new name
**What goes wrong:** a plan adds a `git diff --stat` bound, an allowlist, or a "touch only these files" constraint to feel safe.
**Why it happens:** habit from Phase 16.
**How to avoid:** `D-IMPL-96` revoked it, and `CONTEXT_PRODUCT_REVISION.md` §6.1 records why: *"A constraint needing carve-outs to permit correct work is measuring the wrong thing."* The four anti-leakage lints are the constraint. Criterion 9 is exactly and only those four.
**Warning signs:** a plan whose verification is a diff size.

### Pitfall 6 — Treating the perf battery as out of scope
**What goes wrong:** `make ci` stays green all phase; `make perf` goes red at close, after the baselines' owning plan has merged.
**Why it happens:** `perf` is deliberately not part of `ci` (`Makefile:180`).
**How to avoid:** one task, in the wave that adds the gate or the field, runs `make perf-micro` and records the delta against `perf/thresholds.toml`'s 10%.
**Warning signs:** no plan mentions `perf/`.

### Pitfall 7 — Regenerating a committed baseline in eleven parallel worktrees
**What goes wrong:** `docs/surface/v0.2-surface.json` cannot be concurrently regenerated; `tests/test_surface_drift.py`'s own docstring explains that a bytes-equality gate would be red all phase and *"would get switched off, which is how gates die."*
**How to avoid:** in-flight plans run `tools/dump_surface.py --report` and write a fragment; exactly one plan at phase close regenerates the manifest. Same for `docs/schemas/` if plans run in parallel — though the schemas gate *is* bytes-equality, so schema-touching plans must serialize or each regenerate cleanly.

### Pitfall 8 — Assuming `SkillArgumentSchema` validates anything
**What goes wrong:** a plan says "reuse the proven machinery" and budgets zero for the validator, then discovers no call site validates arguments against a declared schema anywhere in the tree.
**How to avoid:** stated above, with the grep. Budget for a small pure validator, factored once, in the `refuse_attestation` style.

| **Caller reads a product class it is not entitled to** (`build_egress_server` binds no authorizer; scopes are server-wide) | Information Disclosure | Per-class entitlement artifact checked at the door, after authentication, never instead of it. |
| **Bypass field added to an entitlement artifact by a deployment** | Elevation of Privilege | `extra="forbid"` + a field-enumeration test, exactly as `tests/peering/test_trust_policy.py:213` does. |
| **Repudiation of a delivered decision** | Repudiation | Already structural: the replay append *returns* the delivery capability; there is no branch reaching delivery without a recorded decision (`T-04-10`). **Preserve this — do not add a branch to `serve()`.** |
| **A pinned query an auditor cannot resolve** | Repudiation | `QueryPinWriter` persists bytes *before* the record append; `QueryPinMismatch` raised before any store write (`T-15.2-28`). |
| **Payload injection via an unvalidated `Mapping[str, Any]`** | Tampering | The declared payload schema + closed-world `additionalProperties: false`. This is defect 2 and criterion 3. |
| **Debt laundering** — a source outage read as fresh context | Tampering / Info Disclosure | Already mitigated in `FreshnessScorer`: empty observations **raise** rather than scoring 1, *"because letting it enter as perfect freshness would launder a source outage into a low-debt reading."* Any new debt path must preserve this. |
| **Per-principal debt leaking a principal's entitlement scope** (Q7) | Information Disclosure | If debt differs per principal because entitlement differs, a caller can infer *what they are not allowed to see* from the completeness figure. **This is a new threat the design must consider and I have not seen it named anywhere.** Recorded as assumption A4 below and folded into Q7. |

## Open Questions

**These are for Dave's ruling. Do not resolve them in a plan. Question 1 gates the rest.** Each is stated with the options and their consequences so a ruling can be quick.

### Q1 — Is the request surface a typed request contract per product class? **(gating)**

Dave's shape: *"maybe it's just the company name and 'top investors above 25%' or something like that."*

**Evidence in favour, stronger than the phase framing assumed.** Whitepaper §6.3 *Addressability* commits it directly (`docs/CCF_Whitepaper_1-2.md:1465`): *"Addressability gives every product a well-defined interface the agent queries with intent, role, and freshness parameters… the address pattern `/context-products/credit-risk/counterparty/{counterparty-id}?intent=lending-decision&freshness=intraday` encodes exactly what the agent needs: which entity, for what decision, under what temporal requirement."* And `:1465`: *"Structured queries enable decision-time assembly rather than pre-aggregated retrieval."* The `(perspective, context_product_class, selection_params)` plan-selection tuple of `D-IMPL-49` already has a slot for it.

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) Typed request contract per class, declared as a Control Plane artifact** in the `SkillArgumentSchema` shape | Collapses the fixed-subject and fixed-instant defects into one design. Makes a product **callable**. Gives criterion 7 a home, gives discovery something to publish (§6.3's *capability manifest*), and gives addressability a concrete realization. Cost: a new artifact class + schema export + validation at the door + threading request params through to the projection. Largest surface, largest payoff. |
| **(b) No request contract; subject and instant become explicit parameters of the existing serve path** | Much smaller. Fixes the two literals. But leaves the product configurable rather than callable, leaves addressability and discovery with nothing to declare, and Phase 16 documents a product you parameterize by editing YAML. |
| **(c) Defer the contract; add the parameters now, formalize in Phase 17** | Two patches instead of one design — exactly what CONTEXT.md says the contract avoids. Risks a second migration through the same ~30 files. |

**What the answer changes downstream:** Q2 (per-parameter debt only makes sense if parameters are declared), Q5 (one validator or two), Q6 (composition over declared components), criterion 7 and criterion 10 in full.

**Sub-question if (a):** does the request contract's schema shape reuse `SkillArgumentSchema` (one level deep, by ruling) or need nesting? See Q5.

### Q2 — Debt's shape on the wire

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Scalar `D_c` only** | Cheapest; loses the ability to say *which* of F/C/S collapsed — which `DebtAssessment`'s own docstring argues against: *"an examiner asking why a decision was escalated needs to see which of the three dimensions collapsed."* |
| **Per-factor (the shipped `DebtAssessment`: debt + signal + F/C/S)** | Free — the type exists and is already `Decimal`-safe for replay. Does not express Dave's Acme case ("Acme is 3 days old, Omega Securities is 14 months") because F is a mean. |
| **Per-subject recency** (last-observed per entity in the chain) **in addition** | Expresses Dave's worked shape. Cost: the product must carry a per-subject structure, which interacts with the payload schema (is recency payload, or envelope?) and with Q3's determinism constraint. |
| **Per-claim** | Maximum fidelity, maximum size; the whitepaper's passage-level-lineage analogue. Almost certainly Phase 17+. |

**Sub-question: are recency and salience one field or two?** They are mechanically different: recency is a *decay* reading (already computable by `FreshnessScorer`); salience is an *event* reading that can fire while everything is perfectly fresh. The whitepaper's three named debt components (`:2820-2822`) map recency onto F (as-of-time-staleness-debt) and give no home to salience — salience is closest to C (something the decision needs is absent from the assembly) but is not obviously either. **Recommend two fields**, but this is Dave's.

### Q3 — Where does debt land? **(structurally constrained — read § "The hard determinism constraint")**

| Option | Viable? | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Product only** | **No** | `rehydrate_product` cannot reconstruct it; every replay raises `ReplayDivergence`. Not an option. |
| **Replay record only** | Yes | Cheapest, fully deterministic. **Fails criterion 1** and whitepaper `:2696` (*"a runtime signal the agent must consume"*). |
| **Both** | Yes | Satisfies criterion 1 and replay. Cost: the record gains a debt surface, `ReplayEnvironment.rehydrate_product` (a public Protocol) must read it back, and the product's content address changes for every existing example and test. |
| **`ServedProduct` (beside the product) + replay record** | Yes | Debt reaches the consumer on every delivery; the product's bytes — and therefore every content address, A2A signature and recorded expectation — are untouched; replay is unaffected. **Cost: debt does not cross the MCP-egress or A2A wire**, because egress serializes `servable.product` only (`runtime/ccf_runtime/mcp/egress.py:163`). |

*The fourth option is derived from the tree during this research and does not appear in `CONTEXT_PRODUCT_REVISION.md` §9. Offered as an option, not a recommendation.*

### Q4 — What does "a material event transpired" mean mechanically, without domain values in the core?

CONTEXT.md calls this *"the hardest line in the design"* and it is.

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) Opaque salience identifiers**, declared per class in a Control Plane artifact, compared structurally by the fabric and never interpreted | The shipped precedent (`SkillRequirements`, D-IMPL-29: *"The SDK never interprets an identifier"*). Zero domain values in core. Cost: the fabric can only say *"a declared salience condition fired"*, not *why* — the deployment supplies the meaning. |
| **(b) A `SalienceScorerProtocol`** alongside `DebtFactorScorerProtocol`, with the deployment supplying the scorer and its `scorer_id`/`scorer_version` pinned into replay | Consistent with D-IMPL-47's existing pattern for C and S (*"the SDK would otherwise have to invent a required-field vocabulary and a contradiction detector, both of which are deployment knowledge"*). Cost: no reference implementation ships, so the reference example must supply one. |
| **(c) Supersession-as-Policy-event**, reusing the §7.7 mechanism the whitepaper already commits (`λ_c` bounded *"by supersession events as much as by continuous decay"`) | Most whitepaper-faithful; a material event is a supersession of a claim. Cost: requires the supersession path to actually be reachable from the debt computation, which I did **not** verify — see assumption A3. |
| **(d) Defer salience entirely to Phase 17**; ship recency only | Halves the debt work. Costs Dave's worked example, which was the illustration he chose. |

### Q5 — Payload validation: a fourth gate, or a bind at class registration?

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) A fourth validation gate** in `run_gates` | Follows the shipped additive-gate posture exactly (`GATE_SOURCE_ATTESTATION`, keyword-only, `None`-passes, canonical three byte-untouched). Every product is checked on the governed path. Cost: per-serve validation cost (measure against the micro 10% band), and the gate needs the *declared schema* threaded in — either on `RegisteredProductClass` (natural) or as a keyword. |
| **(b) A bind at class registration** — validate that the projection *can* produce the declared shape | Cheaper at serve time. But it validates a *capability*, not an *instance*, and nothing then stops a projection returning a drifted payload at runtime. Weaker than the vessel model's central claim (*"the declared schema IS the discipline"*). |
| **(c) Both** — registration binds the schema, the gate checks each instance | Registration catches misconfiguration at wiring time (the MAX_TTL precedent); the gate catches drift at serve time. Most consistent with everything else in the tree. Highest cost. |

**Sub-question, unavoidable:** `SkillArgumentSchema` is **one level deep by ruling** (`src/ccf/skills/catalog.py:194-206`). A UBO payload with a list of owner objects is not expressible. Choose: extend the shipped class (public-surface change + schema regeneration), add a nesting-capable sibling (two shapes in the tree), or constrain v0 payloads to flat objects (the reference example flattens).

### Q6 — Debt composition weighting for aggregate products

`CONTEXT_PRODUCT_REVISION.md` §4.2: *"It cannot be a max or a mean: components matter differently to the decision, and stale adverse-media may be tolerable where stale ownership is not."*

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) The class declares which components are decision-critical**; debt weights accordingly | The revision's own likely resolution. Keeps values in artifacts. Cost: a declaration format, and a defensible combinator. |
| **(b) Conjunctive over critical components** — take the *worst* debt among components the class marks critical | Consistent with the multiplicative-conjunctive commitment already made for F·C·S (INV-CH7-62/63): *"one weak leg condemns the product however strong the remaining two are."* No weights at all, which sidesteps the `D_c_alt` masking argument entirely. |
| **(c) Multiplicative over all components** | Same conjunctive spirit, but an aggregate of ten components drives `D_c`→1 mechanically regardless of quality — probably too aggressive. |
| **(d) Defer aggregates' debt to Phase 17**; ship lineage union + TTL floor + SLA refusal only (criterion 8's three named limbs) | Criterion 8 as written does **not** mention debt composition. This may already be the intended scope. Worth confirming rather than assuming. |

**Do not ship a weighted-additive combinator in `src/`** whatever the answer — `src/ccf/halflife/debt.py:16-22` forecloses it as an architectural commitment.

### Q7 — The unruled coupling: is debt per-(product, principal)?

If entitlement bounds what an assembler may consult on a caller's behalf, completeness — and therefore debt — differs per principal, so debt cannot be computed once per product.

**The whitepaper supports the premise.** `:1529`: *"two agents operating on the same underlying signals may receive different Context Products because entitlements shape the product, not a view on top of it."* `:1342` calls this *entitlement-formative* and names it one of the five properties that distinguish a Context Product from a Data Product. So different products for different principals is committed; the open part is whether *debt* is one of the things that differs.

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) Yes — debt is computed against the effective entitlement set** | Most faithful to entitlement-formative. `DebtAssessment` becomes a function of `(assembly, profile, effective_entitlements)`. Assembler must record which consultations were **permitted and which refused** (revision §11.3 part 3). Substantially changes the debt work's shape. **New threat, not previously named: a caller can infer what they are not allowed to see from a completeness figure.** Needs a ruling on whether refused-consultation counts are themselves disclosable. |
| **(b) No — debt measures the assembly as delivered; a refused consultation is simply not part of `C_required` for this principal** | Simpler and arguably more correct: if the principal is not entitled to sanctions data, sanctions data is not part of what *their* decision requires, so its absence is not a shortfall. Debt stays one computation per assembled product, and the product is already per-principal because Π and the policy operators are. **This reading dissolves the coupling rather than resolving it** and deserves explicit consideration. |
| **(c) Yes, and debt carries both figures** — debt against the principal's entitled scope *and* a flag that consultations were refused | Most informative, most disclosure-risk. |

**This is the item most likely to change the design's shape and it should be ruled before the debt track is planned**, not during it.

### Q8 — (surfaced by this research, not in CONTEXT.md) Where does `freshness_sla` live?

Whitepaper §7.6 `:2381` says the base's seven fields include **freshness SLA** (per Fig-CH07-13's sidebar) but then unpacks seven fields that include **`plan_id`** and *not* freshness SLA. The two enumerations of "the same seven fields" differ by exactly one element. `D-IMPL-30` separately rules that the Fig-CH07-13 seven-field base is *"a different artifact"* from the specification elements.

| Option | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **(a) Instance field on `ContextProductContract`** | Matches the figure's sidebar. A product carries the SLA it was assembled under. Cost: adds a required field to the vessel with the full ~30-file blast radius, and it must be replay-reconstructible from the product class (it is — that makes it viable). |
| **(b) Class-level on `RegisteredProductClass`** | Matches the docstring's *intent* and D-IMPL-30's "different artifact" framing; makes the docstring true by building what it claims. Cheapest. §6.3's *"Freshness SLA varies by signal and by decision"* suggests a per-signal declaration structure rather than a scalar. |
| **(c) Both** — class declares the promise, instance reports what was achieved | Turns the SLA into something checkable at delivery ("did we meet the promise?") rather than only declarable. Most expensive; arguably the most useful. |

**This is also the resolution point for the criterion-2 count** — nine elements per D-IMPL-30, not eight.
