# Phase 15.5 Plan 14: Phase Close-Out Summary

The phase's own review found two blocking defects in shipped code, both fixed here, and one of them
was found by a reviewer that had to be re-run unsandboxed to see the tree at all.

## What this plan did

Ran the full sweep with every figure recorded, ran the CLAUDE.md §3.1 close-time panel against
**shipped code**, verified every finding against the tree before believing it, fixed the blocking
ones in-phase, regenerated the one baseline the phase deliberately left stale, reconciled the
requirement ledger clause by clause, gave all eight deferred items a disposition, and assembled
`docs/PHASE_15_5_GATE.md`.

## The review — the part that mattered

Three reviewers ran. The briefing followed §3.1's hard-won rule: hand the panel the seven findings
already known and tell it **not to re-derive them**, spend its budget on what is *adjacent*.

**It worked, and the evidence is specific.** The two highest-value findings are the **same finding
reached independently by two reviewers**, and it is exactly the class `DI-15.5-06` names for the
surface manifest — *a check whose subject can be rewritten without changing what the check
observes*. Neither reviewer was asked about AST completeness; both got there from the blind-spot
briefing. This is the second phase running (15.4 was the first) where pointing the panel at the
*neighbourhood* of a known defect produced a better finding than pointing it at the defect.

### P1, because it is the phase's own defect class recurring

`ControlPlaneProductEntitlementSource.authorize` resolved a policy under
`ccf.product-entitlement-policy/<tenant>` and never checked the artifact's own `tenant_id`. The key
and the field are supplied **independently** at all seven registration sites and nothing bound them,
so a policy whose content said tenant B, registered under tenant A's key, authorized A's door with
B's grants — and every downstream leg passed, because the artifact was internally consistent.

It is blocking because `ProductEntitlementPolicy`'s docstring claims tenant-scoping holds *"not by
convention, but because there is no shared object for it to leak through."* That argument is about
content addressing and is true of the artifact; it was not true of the lookup. **A docstring stating
that something is enforced where it is not is the exact defect this phase exists to correct** —
`contract.py` claiming all four class-level §6.3 elements *"live"* on `RegisteredProductClass`,
where only `assembly_mode` did.

### Gemini, rejected in full — and why the rejection is the finding

Its harness never successfully read a file (repeated sub-agent read failures in the transcript, a
`400 INVALID_ARGUMENT` routing loop in stderr) and emitted five confident, well-formatted findings
anyway, **two marked BLOCKING**. Every one cites a symbol that does not exist: `DebtAssessment
.timestamp` (the type has four fields, none a datetime), `CompositeProductAssembler`,
`ProductEntitlementGate`, `VesselAssembler`, `validate_vessel_conformity`,
`tests/invariants/test_import_invariants.py`.

**Second time on this project** — Phase 15.1 rejected one for the same reason. Had the §3.1
verification rule not been applied, this close-out would have spent its remaining budget hunting a
non-existent `utcnow()` in the debt path and a non-existent `jsonschema` import in `products/`.
Recorded as rejected rather than dropped, so the pattern stays visible.

Codex's *first* run is also on the record: under `--sandbox read-only` every shell command died with
`bwrap: loopback: Failed RTM_NEWADDR`, and it **correctly refused to produce uncited findings**
rather than fabricating them. Re-run unsandboxed, it found P1.

## The surface manifest, and what regenerating it does not prove

84 added, 0 removed, 21 reshaped. `tests/test_surface_completeness.py` passes **in both
directions** — every moved symbol has a fragment, every fragment names a moved symbol — across ten
`15.5-NN` fragments. This was the one plan in the phase permitted to regenerate it; a bytes-equality
gate cannot be concurrently regenerated, and `test_surface_drift.py`'s own docstring says a gate
left red all phase *"would get switched off, which is how gates die."*

**And it is not evidence of surface stability for this phase.** Confirmed by reading the regenerated
file, not inferred from `D-IMPL-110`: neither `ProductResolverProtocol` nor
`ExperienceProductResolverProtocol` appears among the 21 reshaped names, though both went from three
arguments to five — a break for every adopter supplying a resolver. `dump_surface.py` reads a
Protocol's `__init__`, so `__call__` is invisible. 56 entries record `(*args, **kwargs)`, 30 of them
`*Protocol`, against 54 Protocol classes. Phase 17 owns it.

### Deliberate non-fixes

**`refuse_bound_payload` / `refuse_request` were not wired into the doors.** Two panelists called
this blocking. It is Rule 4 territory: the placement is `D-IMPL-102`'s own — `bind.py:40-48` states
the module *"takes neither a request nor a door"* — and wiring it makes each door resolve a
`RegisteredProductClass` per serve and render a new refusal, putting a deployment-declared verdict
inside the SDK's delivery path, adjacent to the line limb 3 draws. Surfaced as residual R1 and
`DI-15.5-05` rather than decided by an executor.

**`ProductEntitlementPolicy`'s docstring was not reworded.** Editing it moves a committed schema's
`description` bytes for a precision gain. Residual R5.
