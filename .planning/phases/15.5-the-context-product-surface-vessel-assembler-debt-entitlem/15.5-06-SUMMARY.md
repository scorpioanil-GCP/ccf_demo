## Decisions Made

**1. The shared seams live in a third package, not in `egress.py`.** The plan says twice to import `PerspectiveResolverProtocol` **from `ccf_runtime.mcp.egress`** into `experience.py` and `product.py`. That instruction is unexecutable as written: `tests/invariants/test_mcp_role_separation.py::test_the_experience_role_is_its_own_module` asserts `egress.__name__ not in _imported_names(experience)`, and `test_neither_mcp_server_role_imports_the_other` asserts the same for the artifact role — the invariant exists so that "the authenticated one is never one decorator away from the anonymous one".

The plan's *intent* — three doors, one definition of "entitled" — is fully preserved and arguably better served by `ccf_runtime.authz.product_entitlement`: no door is privileged over another, and the shape is the one `ccf_runtime.authz.protocol` already models for the two HITL doors, which likewise never import each other. The plan's `key_links` pattern `from ccf_runtime.mcp.egress import` is therefore not satisfied literally; the property it was proxying for is asserted directly, in `test_the_front_door_imports_the_seams_rather_than_re_declaring_them` and in the parameterised door test.

**2. The entitlement decides on the resolved role.** The plan's stated handler order is `require_role` → construct `Perspective`. Implemented instead as: resolve the perspective (a pure derivation over the verified grant that touches no fabric and serves nothing) → refuse a substituted subject → decide on the **resolved** role and the requested class. This is strictly stronger: checking only a *requested* role passes against a resolver that ignores its argument, which is exactly the T-15.5-19 hole. It also keeps the audit at one event per request. `test_a_resolver_that_mints_an_ungranted_role_is_refused` is the case that distinguishes the two orders.

**3. "No implicit role" is realized by refusing, not by defaulting.** The plan's behaviour list says a caller requesting no role "receives the grant's declared default only if the policy declares one; otherwise it is refused". 15.5-03's shipped `ProductEntitlementGrant` declares **no** default-role field — `permitted_roles` is a set — so the SDK has no default to hand out and does not invent one. With `requested_role=None` the deployment's resolver derives a role from the verified grant, and that role is checked against `permitted_roles` like any other. Adding a default-role field to 15.5-03's contract would be an architectural change to another plan's artifact and was not made.

**4. Required on MCP, optional on HTTP.** Both MCP builders raise on an absent governance seam. The HTTP route's seams are optional, because authentication on that surface is itself optional by ruling (D-IMPL-73's acknowledged insecure default) and a required seam would make a still-supported posture unbuildable. `create_app` warns with a new `CcfDeadEntitlementWarning` in the WR-05 shape when the seams are bound without `auth=`, and `build_router` raises when exactly one of the two is supplied.

**5. The role reaches the doors differently, and the Protocol is uniform anyway.** The MCP product URIs carry three segments and no role; adding a fourth would change what a product URI *means* on a surface whose siblings address the same three facts. So both MCP doors call the resolver with `requested_role=None` and the HTTP route supplies an optional `?role=` query parameter. One Protocol signature, two ways of reaching it.

## Known Stubs

**One, and it is a scope boundary rather than a stub in the usual sense — recorded rather than left to be inferred.**

`entitled_perspective` returns the `Perspective` the caller is entitled to, and the two MCP doors discard the return value. They are not *ignoring* it: the perspective is the **subject of the entitlement decision**, and the decision is what the doors enforce — a caller not entitled to the class, or to the role the deployment's resolver derives for it, is refused before any product is resolved. What the doors do not do is *forward* that perspective into the assemble-and-gate path, because `ProductResolverProtocol` is a three-string seam and the deployment's resolver constructs its own `Perspective` behind it.

Closing that gap means reshaping `ProductResolverProtocol` and `ExperienceProductResolverProtocol` to carry a `Perspective`, which touches `ApiDependencies`, the CLI's `--local` composition, three doors, two test children, and three examples — roughly twenty call sites. **The plan does not ask for it**; its own `<interfaces>` block quotes the resolver seam unchanged at all three doors, and its success criterion is that the perspective is *derived from the verified grant and never asserted by the caller*, which is satisfied. It is stated in both MCP module docstrings and in `deferred-items.md` so that a later reader does not have to infer why the value stops there.
