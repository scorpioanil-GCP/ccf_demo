<objective>
Turn the phase's central claim into a script that can fail.

"The SDK needs no refactor to accept a new substrate" is normally an
unfalsifiable architectural assertion. Reduced to *"adding a real substrate
touches one new module, one writer class, and a bounded one-line extension of
four existing enumerations — no mechanism anywhere in the SDK changed,"* it
becomes a statement about a diff, and therefore mechanically checkable. **That
reduction is the phase's intellectual product as much as the adapter is**, which
is why the script is a deliverable and why its implementation is reviewable
rather than autonomous.

This plan implements **`D-IMPL-92` as corrected**. Read the entry and its
2026-08-08 correction before writing a line. The ruling is stated in two layers
on purpose: a **normative** rule (the named registration files, each scoped to
its named block, everything else under `src/ccf` still restricted) and the
**mechanical proxy** the script actually runs. Both are recorded together so a
reader can see which is the claim and which is its approximation, and the budgets
are an admitted proxy for "extension, not modification" — to be tightened to a
direct AST check if any future phase finds them gameable.

Two things the gate must **not** become. It must not move into `tools/lints`:
D-10 is explicit that it asserts a fact about one phase's diff, not a standing
invariant, so it copies the *output contract* and not the location. And it must
not build a backend registration seam. `D-IMPL-92` records that as a
well-evidenced future candidate it **explicitly does not commission** —
constructing it inside the phase whose whole claim is "no refactor was needed"
would be precisely the refactor the claim denies. The same reasoning forbids
splitting `src/ccf/graph/_writer.py` into a package: that is a carried advisory
(15.2 close-review A5) with no `DI-15.2-NN` entry, and layer-2 rule (4) would
fail on the phase's own refactor.

Output: `tools/gates/gate_15_3.py`, `make gate-15.3`, the scheduled drift job,
and the committed gate package.
