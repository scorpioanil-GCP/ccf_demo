## A near-miss on MY OWN work, recorded because the lesson is reusable

**Dave asked for this kept in the record.** While verifying this wave's ledger
edit, a reviewer grepped each row for `Phase 24|DOCS- lane` and took the FIRST
match. It reported **all six rows still pointing at Phase 24** — which would
have meant Dave's repoint ruling had been ignored. **It had not been.** The
finding was false and was caught only by printing the cells and reading them.

**Diagnosed to two compounding faults, measured rather than described:**

1. **The second alternative NEVER MATCHED.** The spelling `DOCS- lane` occurs at **no position in any cell**; the ledger's spelling is `` the `DOCS-` requirement lane of the v0.3 milestone ``. So `Phase 24|DOCS- lane` silently degraded into a **`Phase 24`-only scan** — a false-absence instrument, the same shape this project has now found in its own machinery nine waves running. An alternation whose second branch never fires reports the first branch everywhere and looks exactly like a finding.
2. **First-match over a whole cell is unsound here even when spelled correctly.** Measured offsets in the current tree: `DOCS-10` carries its current inheritor at **1444** and the superseded `Phase 24` at **1580**; `DOCS-03` carries the lane at **981** and `Phase 24` at **1356** and **1509**. A correctly-spelled alternation would have returned the RIGHT answer on both — **by the luck of my drafting order, not by soundness.** A row drafted the other way round reads wrong, silently.

**The generalizable rule, which is the point of recording this:**

> **In this ledger, a token's PRESENCE in a cell is not evidence of that token's
> CURRENT force, because the cells are deliberately written to keep superseded
> values readable.** Any instrument over these cells must anchor on the
> disposition sentence — `**DISPOSITION (Phase 23.1, criterion 1):** …
> Inheritor named per criterion 2: …` — and read the clause that follows it.
> It must never scan the cell.

**This hazard is created by a convention that is itself correct.** Preserving
superseded reasoning inline is what makes the ledger auditable — it is why a
reader can see that `DOCS-10` was pointed at Phase 24 by Wave 2 and repointed by
Dave, rather than seeing only the current answer with no history. **The
convention is not the defect; a cell-scanning reader is.** Naming it as a hazard
the convention creates for later readers, rather than as a reason to stop
preserving history.

**The anchored reader, demonstrated.** Anchoring on the disposition sentence and
printing the clause that follows returns, for the seven rows carrying an
inheritor: `EXAM-01`, `EXAM-03`, `DOCS-11`, `DOCS-12`, `DOCS-13` →
**`Phase 24 (Context Production)`**; `DOCS-03`, `DOCS-10` →
**the `DOCS-` requirement lane of the v0.3 milestone**. That is the ruling as
Dave gave it. **The naive reader's control is the finding itself:** it returned
`Phase 24` for all seven, including the two it should not have.
