<why_this_lands_late_rather_than_early>

this phase adds.

1. Every control this phase ships exists by the end of plan `21.1-06`. Landing the registry earlier
   would freeze it over a partial set and force an amendment in every later wave — and an artefact

   `_refuse` performs on refusal ids.

`CONTROL 13` declared as `pathspec` aims and NOT yet re-aimed; the meta-control that checks pathspec
aims lands in plan `21.1-08`, in the same plan as their repair, committed BEFORE the repair so it is
observed RED naming exactly those two. This plan's own two meta-controls get their RED from

<criterion_13_does_not_fit_every_control_and_this_is_the_reading>
**A defect in criterion 13 as written.** It says every CONTROL's "mutation is watched RED on the
aimed limb and GREEN when that limb is intact". Four of the module's controls have no mutation and no
limb: they are POSITIVE twins whose job is that a compliant subject is ACCEPTED — the positive
pathspec control at `:550`, `CONTROL 9b`, `CONTROL 17b`, and the positive citation control at `:1460`.
"Red on everything is not a check" is their whole purpose, and each of their docstrings says so.

Criterion 13 read literally either excludes them — which is the narrowing-a-claim-to-fit-a-weak-control
move this phase exists to stop — or is unsatisfiable.

**The reading:** the registry carries a third aim kind, `discriminator`, whose proof obligation is
INVERTED. A discriminator is proven by forcing the predicate it discriminates against to refuse
EVERYTHING and requiring the control to go red. A discriminator that stays green while its subject
predicate refuses everything is discriminating nothing. Put the reading to Dave at the close.
</criterion_13_does_not_fit_every_control_and_this_is_the_reading>

<the_registry_schema_decision>
**The entry carries a FROZENSET of targets, not one target. The decision is taken here rather than
left to the executor, because the cheap way out of both cases below is to drop a limb from `LIMBS` or
narrow a control's claim — which is the narrowing this phase exists to stop, and which `_refuse` would
reject anyway the moment that branch fired with an unregistered id.**

Two measured cases force it, and a single-target registry is red on landing for both:

1. **`test_control_c_an_empty_path_list_fails_loudly` (`:439-454`) is ONE test looping over TWO
   refusals** — `fabric_diff`'s empty-base limb (`:243`) and its no-paths limb (`:248`). Both are
   limbs under plan `21.1-05`, and NO other test exercises either. With one target per control, one
   of the two is unclaimed, `CONTROL 34`'s coverage limb reddens the moment it lands, and it stays
   red through waves 8, 9 and 10 — breaking `make ci` and falsifying criterion 10.
2. **`CONTROL A` vouches for FOUR assertions** — `FABRIC`, `FENCE`, `UAT_RECORDS` and `PINNED_FILES` —
   and `RECON-08`'s shipped ledger cell repeats that claim verbatim. With one target, the pathspec-aim
   proof in plan `21.1-08` would establish that `CONTROL A` reaches ONE of the four while it satisfies
   its registry entry and remains unproven for three. That is `RR-9` living inside the meta-control
   built to end `RR-9`.

**Consequences, and they are what make the frozenset honest rather than decorative:**

- coverage is over the UNION of every entry's target set, so a limb is claimed only if some entry
  actually names it;
- the aim proof quantifies over the SET: for EACH target independently, that one target is neutered
  (or that one constant corrupted) and the control is required to fail. A control that survives with
  one of its targets broken has not been proven for that target, whatever it proves for the others;
- `CONTROL C` is NOT split into two tests to fit the registry. Its docstring calls its two subjects
  "the two other ways to compare nothing and call it clean", and they belong together.
</the_registry_schema_decision>

<meta_is_a_regress_stopper_not_a_filing_cabinet>
**`guard` is a real fifth kind and `CONTROL 32`/`33` take it, not `meta`.** Found at plan-check, and
the asymmetry is the whole argument.

`meta` entries are excluded from every invocation loop by design — `CONTROL 35` iterates `limb`,
`CONTROL 36` iterates `discriminator`, `CONTROL 38` iterates `pathspec`, and none of them touches a
`meta` entry. For the aim-proving controls themselves that exclusion is legitimate: you cannot prove
the prover forever, and the regress has to stop somewhere it can be seen stopping.

`CONTROL 32` and `CONTROL 33` are not provers. They are ordinary controls over two ordinary in-module
guards — the dispatcher's unregistered-limb check, and the fixture asserting the neutering switch is
off. Filing them under `meta` would leave them registered, enumerated by the coverage limb, and
**aim-unproven**, and they are the two whose silent failure makes every other control in this module
vacuous. That is not stopping a regress; it is dodging the taxonomy.

So `guard` carries an inverted obligation, quantified in a standing loop exactly as `discriminator`
is: **sabotage the guarded mechanism, require the control to fail, restore, require it to pass.**
Both saboteurs are already written in plan `21.1-06` task 3.
</meta_is_a_regress_stopper_not_a_filing_cabinet>

<selector_discipline>

plans: the control renumbering left seven `<verify>` selectors pointing at already-landed,
already-green controls — including the one guarding the strongest piece of evidence the phase

That is this phase's defect class, in this phase's own verification commands.

So every task below that adds a control carries a SELECTOR PROOF beside its run: for each control

among the collected node ids. Two lines of shell, and its absence is exactly what let seven wrong
selectors ship.
</selector_discipline>
