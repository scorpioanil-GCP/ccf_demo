## Summary

The subject is one module. It is well-built and unusually self-aware: it carries 24 named controls, refuses on nine distinct branches, and its docstrings state its own defect class in its own words. The three blocking findings are all instances of that class arriving inside the machinery written to catch it — a guard whose *aggregate* passes on behalf of a broken *member* (RR-1), two readers that disagree about what a cell says with neither refusing (RR-2), and a ruling document whose scope decision lives only in a wave hand-off (RR-3).

Every one of the six briefed findings reproduced exactly as briefed. Beyond them, this survey measured something the panel did not reach: **eight of the module's refusal branches can be deleted outright and all 51 tests still pass.** Two of those eight are not merely unfalsified but structurally *unreachable* — `refuse_unresolvable`'s zero-row limb is preceded by `traceability_rows`' own refusal, and `uncited_verdicts`' empty-roster limb is never handed an empty roster by any caller. That is the same class again, one level below RR-9, and it is the widest form of the thing this phase was commissioned to close.

The blast radius is the good news, and it is measured rather than estimated. All five fixes were simulated together as in-memory patches and the shipped suite ran **51 passed** — unchanged. No existing assertion goes red. In particular: every member of all six pathspec constants matches at least one tracked file, so per-member validation is a no-op on the current tree; and **zero of the 143 real ledger rows are flagged by the cell-level single-verdict rule**, so RR-2's fix requires no ledger edit and surfaces no hidden bad row. The one alternative rule shape that *would* have forced ledger edits — "the leading verdict must be bolded" — flags 69 real rows and must not be chosen.

**Primary recommendation:** fix all five in the module, quantify per member rather than per tuple, define a cell's verdict as `{leading token} ∪ {bolded vocabulary words}` and refuse when that set has more than one member, and re-aim every non-vacuity control at the constant the assertion it vouches for actually passes — using a base derived per member from `git log -1 --format=%H -- <member>`, which is available for all 21 members and refuses by construction on a typo.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: fixing the aggregate and calling the member closed
**What goes wrong:** `B-1` added `tracked_under` and the write-up said the hole was closed. It was closed for arity 1.
**How to avoid:** for every new refusal, ask *what is the smallest broken part of my subject that this predicate still accepts?*
**Warning sign:** a truthiness test applied to a collection.

### Pitfall 2: a control that is green and was never red
**What goes wrong:** `CONTROL 13` was recorded in `21-06-SUMMARY.md:144` as *"GREEN, non-vacuously"* — never observed failing. `D-IMPL-170` is explicit that this is not a control.
**How to avoid:** criterion 8. Run every new control against the **pre-fix** module and record the RED; then against the post-fix module and record the GREEN.

### Pitfall 3: a control whose subject is refused by an earlier branch
**What goes wrong:** wave 1 lost `CONTROL 4` and `CONTROL 4b` this way; this survey found `CONTROL 5` and `refuse_unresolvable:1202` in the same state.
**How to avoid:** before writing a control, hand its exact subject to the shipped function and confirm which branch fires.

### Pitfall 4: a git-diff control run against an uncommitted tree
**What goes wrong:** `fabric_diff` compares COMMITS. A working-tree edit is invisible to it, so a control run before committing reports the wrong thing.
**How to avoid:** commit, then run. Stated at `:238-239` and in the orchestrator's constraints.

### Pitfall 5: choosing a verdict rule that re-opens ruled rows
**What goes wrong:** the "any vocabulary word anywhere" rule flags 13 real rows including `DCD-09`, `DCD-10` and `CPS-03` — all ruled by Dave. `D-09` fixed the vocabulary at six words precisely to prevent this.
**How to avoid:** the union rule. Measured at 0 of 143.

### Pitfall 6: editing `.planning/STATE.md` or `ROADMAP.md` with a GSD writer
**What goes wrong:** every `state.*` and `roadmap.*` writer drops frontmatter fields and corrupts both files, whether it reports success or failure.
**How to avoid:** `D-15`. Back up, hand-edit, diff.

### Pitfall 7: forgetting the generated report
**What goes wrong:** `docs/surface/examples/RETIREMENT-COUPLINGS.md` walks `.planning/`; every planning commit staleness-breaks CI.
**How to avoid:** regenerate LAST, commit it, then check.
