## What shipped

### The carrier, `qa-toplevel` (`D-IMPL-118`)

`ContextProductContract` gained `debt_evidence: DebtEvidence`, defaulted to
`NO_DEBT_EVIDENCE` — **a sibling of `debt`, not a member of `DebtAssessment`**.
The ruled clause implemented, quoted:

> *"The ruling forgoes that free ride deliberately, buying the field its own
> identity on the vessel rather than making it a rider on debt."*

The element is `DebtEvidencePointer`, a `CCFContract` carrying one opaque
`handle: str` in the `SkillRequirements` voice — no `Literal`, no `StrEnum`, no
URI type, no vocabulary, no default. The collection is a **sorted tuple** rather
than a `frozenset`: the `scorers` precedent, because the value is inside a
content address and must be order-insensitive in its bytes — and because a set
would collapse a repeated handle *silently*, when the repetition is exactly the
incoherence worth refusing out loud.

`D-IMPL-118` is explicit that half of this is not an implementation:

> *"An executor of any of the three plans that ships the vessel field without
> the record-side member and the seven rehydrator edits has not implemented this
> ruling."*

### `D-IMPL-119` — limb 3(b), taken explicitly

**The limb taken: 3(b) — omit evidence pointers entirely.** The ruled clause,
quoted verbatim and named by entry number as the acceptance criterion requires:

> **`D-IMPL-119`, limb 3(b):** *"**omit evidence pointers entirely** until a
> later phase adds principal scoping to the assembly path."*

**Why (b) rather than (a).** Limb 1 makes the safety property **the SDK's**, not
the adopter's — the ruling declined `qb2-opaque` precisely because that *"moves
a real disclosure question onto every adopter, and the SDK cannot then say the
surface is safe by construction."* Limb 4 then refuses a handle that is merely
*encoded*: *"an obfuscated `entity_uri` is an interpretable pointer with an
extra step."* Limb 2 puts principal scoping out of scope for this phase. The
only inputs in hand at the shipped population site are the assembled
`EntityClaim`s, so every handle derivable there is either interpretable —
refused by limb 4 — or has had its referent discarded, which is a pointer that
points at nothing wearing the costume of one that does. Emitting the named
sentinel says the same thing without the costume.

**Where it is written for an adopter**, not only here:
`examples/s1-01-hello-context-product/deployment.py::KycScenario.debt_evidence`
carries the full reasoning and the quoted clause, and the construction site in
that example's `spine.py` names the limb beside the field. The method takes the
`GraphView` and does not read it, deliberately: the signature is the one a
populating deployment needs, so the phase that closes the scoping gap changes a
body and not a call site.

**What is not asserted:** that scoped derivation is unwanted. The ruling
forecloses that reading itself, and the summary records it rather than leaving
it inferable.

**On the `D-IMPL-105` interaction:** nothing shipped here varies by principal —
neither an omitted pointer nor the sentinel does — so the coupling that ruling
dissolved does not arise.

### On task 2's acceptance criterion about a `path::Symbol`

The criterion is conditional — *"if `D-IMPL-119` constrained derivation to the
perspective-scoped assembly, … the summary names the symbol the scoped set is
read from"*. **The antecedent does not hold.** Limb 2 states this phase
introduces no perspective-scoped view into the assembly path, and the ruling
resolves the criterion itself: *"there is no such symbol, so the task names the
limb it took — (a) or (b) — and shows … that no pointer is emitted."* Named
above: **(b)**.

## Findings

**1. The census's count survives the ruling; its mechanism does not.** The
`skill-set in force` hash on the line directly below the one that moved
(`aafa91413bcd8b21…`) **did not move**, and the census predicted it would. The
prediction was correct for `qa-nested`, where `DebtAssessment` itself reshapes
and everything carrying one moves with it. Under the ruled `qa-toplevel` the
assessment is byte-identical, so only the record address — carrying
`ContentGenerationPath`, which gained the member — moves. Recorded in the
fragment so a later plan does not re-derive it.

**2. There are no consequential `ccf.hitl` entries, and the plan budgeted
four.** Task 3's acceptance criterion required four `ccf.hitl.*` entries reading
*"consequential, not deliberate"*, on 15.5-11's precedent. That criterion was
written for the placement `D-IMPL-118` declined: under `qa-toplevel`
`DebtAssessment` gains no field, so nothing whose `trigger` may be one moves. A
fresh-manifest-versus-committed diff shows **zero** `ccf.hitl` movement.
Claiming four consequential entries would have been a **false claim in a
migration note**, so the fragment says why they are absent instead. This is a
deviation from the plan's literal criterion in favour of the ruling, per the
standing "where the plan text and a ruling disagree, the ruling wins".

**3. The census sweep now reports one unclassified file:**
`docs/DEBT_COMPLETENESS_RULING_EVIDENCE.md`, which post-dates the classification
table and holds the spike's own stand-in measurement addresses. It is **not** a
pin site and was **not** regenerated — editing it would falsify the measurement
record the rulings were made against. Flagged for `15.6-12`, which retires the
spike.

**4. `docs/DECISIONS.md` needs no correction.** `D-IMPL-118`, `D-IMPL-119` and
`D-IMPL-120` were all read before implementation and none was found wrong or
stale against the tree. (`D-IMPL-120`'s quoted `loc: subject_uri` is the spike's
stand-in name; the shipped field is `subject`, and the entry is quoting the
measurement rather than naming the shipped field — not a defect.)
