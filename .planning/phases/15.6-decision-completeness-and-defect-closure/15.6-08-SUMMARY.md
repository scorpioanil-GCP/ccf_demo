## What shipped

### The binding, and the line it does not cross

`DebtProfile` gained `signal_obligations: SignalObligations` (defaulted to
`NO_SIGNAL_OBLIGATIONS`) and one method, `obligation_for(signal) -> str`. The
element is `SignalObligation`, a `CCFContract` with **exactly two** members:
`signal: DebtSignal` and `obligation_ref: str`.

**Keys validated, values never.** A key that is not a `DebtSignal` member is
refused at construction with the offending key in the message — the SDK is
entitled to that check because `DebtSignal` is a whitepaper §7.7 CAR type it
owns, ships and already branches on. The value gets no such treatment:
`test_the_sdk_accepts_an_obligation_string_it_has_never_seen` passes for
`urn:acme:obligation:9f2c-never-seen-by-the-sdk`.

Three refusals that are *structural*, not judgements about meaning: a duplicate
signal, a blank reference (a half-declared binding must not be confusable with
no binding), and the sentinel's own spelling (nothing may impersonate it).

**What is absent on purpose, and asserted as absent:** no obligation type, no
`StrEnum` or `Literal` of obligation names, no default mapping, no reference
constant, no permitted set, and no branch anywhere in `src/` or `runtime/` that
reads an obligation and acts on it.

### Why this member's seam is stronger than its two siblings' — the false free ride

The plan asserted that *"because `ContentGenerationPath.debt` already carries
the whole assessment, the obligation rides for free."* **Under `qa-toplevel`
that premise is false**, and the plan's own instruction — *confirm that by
running, not by reading* — is what caught it: the replay case set failed until
the `ContentGenerationPath` member and the seven rehydrator edits were done.
Nothing was weakened to make it pass.

The distinction worth recording is sharper than "not free". For `debt_evidence`
and `source_refresh_recency`, a rehydrator that omits the read-back has
**nowhere else to get the value** — `ProjectionOutput` carries exactly
`(plan_id, payload)`. For this member the alternative is not omission but
**re-resolution**: a rehydrator holds the deployment's `DebtProfile` and can look
the obligation up again for the same signal, producing something entirely
plausible and answering *"what was this decision obliged to do?"* with **today's**
binding. That is `D-IMPL-26`'s exact-version rule violated, and it is the reason
the resolved value travels on the product.

Three cases prove it on the real serve path:

1. `test_a_replay_reproduces_the_obligation_in_force_at_decision_time` — serves
   under one calibration, **rebinds the profile in the deployment's Control
   Plane**, replays, and asserts **both directions**: the recorded obligation
   reproduces *and* the current one does not. Asserting only the first would
   pass against a rebinding that never took effect.
2. `test_a_rehydration_that_resolves_todays_binding_is_divergence` — the
   substitution attempted and refused. Strictly stronger than the drop case: the
   returned product carries a *plausible* obligation, so only the content
   address distinguishes it.
3. `test_what_the_signal_obliged_replays_byte_exactly` — run after the graph has
   moved on four months, recomputing **both** addresses rather than trusting
   either.

Plus the rest of the established set: on the vessel and not in the assessment;
dimension 5 with no sixth minted (INV-CH7-80, by field enumeration); a dropped
obligation as divergence; and a pre-seam record still replaying under the
sentinel.

### `[Ruling wins]` `assess_debt` gained no parameter and the assessment gained no member

The plan's task-2 behaviour reads *"`assess_debt` returns an assessment carrying
the obligation the profile bound for the signal it computed"* and its `key_links`
say *"assess_debt resolves the binding … and carries it onto the assessment"*.
**That is the `qa-nested` shape `D-IMPL-118` declined.** Under `qa-toplevel` the
member is a top-level sibling of `debt` on the vessel, so hanging it on
`DebtAssessment` would have moved the assessment's bytes — reshaping everything
that carries one — for a member the ruling put somewhere else.

The surviving requirements are satisfied at the correct sites: the resolution is
`DebtProfile.obligation_for(signal)`, on the artifact that owns the binding; the
vessel is the populate site; a plain attribute read extracts.
`test_assess_debt_is_unchanged_by_the_obligation_binding` asserts `debt` and
`signal` are byte-identical for the same factors whether or not the profile
carries bindings, and that the assessment's field set is untouched.

**No `debt.py` wrapper was added** around `obligation_for`. Two spellings of one
resolution is two things to drift.

### `[Ruling wins]` A sorted tuple, not a mapping

The plan's behaviour repeatedly says *"mapping"*, and also requires that *"a
duplicate key is refused"*. Those cannot both hold for a YAML mapping: pyyaml
keeps the **last** of two duplicate keys silently. The declaration is therefore a
**list of `{signal, obligation_ref}` entries**, normalized to a signal-unique,
canonically ordered tuple — which also mirrors `SourceRefreshRecency` exactly
and gives the order-insensitivity the content address requires.

### `[Rule 1 — in scope]` The sentinel is `ccf.`-namespaced

`NO_OBLIGATION_IN_FORCE` was first written as `"no-obligation-in-force"`, on the
`NO_PAYLOAD_SCHEMA` precedent. A pre-existing guard —
`tests/halflife/test_debt_delivery.py::test_every_module_level_string_constant_is_structural`
— requires every module-level string in `ccf/halflife` to be an SDK-namespaced
identifier, a semver, or the mode token. **That guard is right**, so the sentinel
became `"ccf.no-obligation-in-force"`, which also makes an accidental collision
by a deployment implausible.

### `[Rule 1 — in scope]` Two field-enumeration guards fired and were updated

`tests/products/test_request_contract.py::test_no_content_address_moves_because_of_the_pin`
and
`tests/replay/test_harness_trace.py::test_every_pre_change_field_is_byte_for_byte_unchanged`
both failed on the new field — **which is what they are for**. Both were updated
to name `obligation_in_force` with the ruling behind it; the frozen
`PRE_CHANGE_CANONICAL` literal was **not** retouched, only a new sentinel-key
`.replace()` step added, so the captured literal still stands as evidence.

### `[Rule 3 — blocking]` A stale line-number citation was re-cited by symbol

See Finding 5. `docs/SURFACE-AUDIT.md` is outside every manifest; the edit was
one citation form and changed no claim.
