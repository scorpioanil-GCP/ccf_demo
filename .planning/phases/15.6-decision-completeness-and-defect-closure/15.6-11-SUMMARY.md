## The framing, first, because a later reader will otherwise assume the opposite

**The citation gate was GREEN throughout.** `python3 -m tools.lints --citations`
reported **0 findings** at this plan's base commit and reports **0 findings**
now. An *unanchored* citation is not a failing citation — it is one the checker
declines to check for want of an anchor. This plan raised **coverage**; it did
not repair a red gate. `tests/invariants/test_citation_coverage.py` says so in
the first paragraph of its own docstring and in the opening clause of every one
of its five failure messages.

rebuilt.** `tools/lints/citations.py::RULE_NODE_ID` implements `cite03-node-id`

its target does not define and asserts a `cite03-node-id` finding comes back —
because a claim in a SUMMARY nobody re-reads is not a discharge.

## Findings

### 1. The checker caught a guessed symbol, which is the whole point

`docs/CONTEXT_PRODUCT_RULING_EVIDENCE.md` said *"Where supersession actually
lives | `src/ccf/graph/protocols.py:132-133` — `ClaimRecord.superseded_by` /
`superseded_at`"*. Converting the anchor to the type the prose named produced a
`cite03-node-id` finding: **`protocols.py` defines no `ClaimRecord`**, and
`git log -S "class ClaimRecord" -- src/ccf/graph/protocols.py` returns **nothing
— it has never existed in that module**. The cited *lines* were right
(`EntityRecord.superseded_by` / `superseded_at`, with the same pair on
`RelationshipRecord`); the *type name* was wrong when written. The row now names
both real classes and records the correction with its evidence. **A guessed
symbol resolves and points at the wrong thing, which is worse than a stale
anchor — and the only reason this one was caught in seconds is that the sweep
was validated by the gate rather than by reading.**

### 4. Two dispositions, deliberately different

**Live pointers** — a citation a reader is meant to follow — became
`path::Symbol`, or a bare path where the cited region is a module docstring and
no symbol encloses it.

**Quoted past defects** — the five entries in
`.planning/phases/15.1-.../deferred-items.md` of the form *"cited X for Y, which
is at Z"* — were **marked historical, never re-pointed**. Re-anchoring a record
of what a wrong citation said destroys the record. The disposition is
`tools/lints/citations.py`'s own ruled mechanism: *"A citation deliberately
scoped to a past state of the tree is correct and must not be flagged"*, made
mechanical by `HISTORICITY_MARKERS`. The `historical` count rose 11 → 18
accordingly, and this matches `15.6-09`'s established pattern verbatim: *"A
quoted-stale anchor is marked HISTORICAL, never anchored."*

### 5. One citation deliberately left alone

`.planning/ROADMAP.md`'s criterion 5 and its quotation in `docs/SURFACE-AUDIT.md`
both carry a **bare continuation** `` `:244-250` `` with no path. It is invisible
to the checker by design (`citations.py` names bare continuations among what it
cannot see) and it currently lands inside `refuse_attestation`, which is plainly
not what it meant. **I could not determine what it originally named, so I did not
invent one** — the plan's own warning is that a guessed symbol looks correct.
Recorded here for the gate.
