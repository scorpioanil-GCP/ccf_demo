## Phase Boundary

Make the SDK deliver what `D-IMPL-251` ruled: *"CCF refusal is refusal of fulfillment, not refusal
of response."* Every valid invocation of the delivery surface returns a governed Context Product
response; a refusal is the shell form of that response and participates in identity, policy,
contract and Decision Replay; exceptions are reserved for the fabric's own failure to reach a
determination. The phase (a) introduces the policy-constraint layer and SDK-applied product
defaults, with provenance of authority preserved through resolution; (b) applies the five
`D-IMPL-251` ticks to debt, empty results and the identity refusal; (c) extends the refusal shell
and Decision Replay so a request that produced no product is a first-class replayable decision;
(d) makes the HTTP and MCP doors transport governed refusals as governed responses and reserve
transport errors for CCF failure; (e) rebuilds and republishes cp-04 on the new depth semantics;
(f) consumes the entire carry roster of `D-GATE-24.5.1` (B-01..B-14), the five ledger candidates
(`LC-01..05`) and the two rows `D-IMPL-245` opened by name.

**Not a Context Product rung** (`D-IMPL-252`). cp-05 enters discussion only after `D-GATE-24.6`
with no fix rows open (`D-IMPL-235` (5)); its number is not assigned.

**Fence.** `src/` and `runtime/` OPEN. `examples/` and `tests/examples/` open only where an SDK
change makes a rung's behavior, prose, transcript or page stale; the named case is cp-04. The
24.5.1 discipline stands: back-correct examples to the SDK that now exists; do not back-correct
history; do not implement the next SDK inside an example. Widening the fence is a dated
`D-IMPL` before the edit (`D-IMPL-214`).

**Dave's framing of the six areas (2026-09-04):** *"They are not competing alternatives; together
they define the 24.6 boundary work we just agreed to."* Priority order: policy-constraint layer
(product defaults folded in — adjacent but conceptually distinct, not a fifth workstream) → debt
and empty results (*"Apply D-IMPL-251 rather than reopen it"*) → refusal shell and replay → doors
→ cp-04 depth → ledger fence.

**Design caution, his words:** *"don't begin with Pydantic classes. Start with the
authority/resolution semantics and a few worked cases."* The worked cases are in `<specifics>`.

**The transport rule, his words:** *"Governed determination → governed response. Technical/runtime
failure → transport error."*

**THE INVARIANT (`D-IMPL-254`, Dave, 2026-09-04) — binds every decision below:** *"Internal truth,
governing authority, and permitted disclosure are distinct concerns. The fabric preserves the
governed truth of why a determination was reached; Policy determines what of that truth may cross
the consumer boundary; the response shell expresses only the permitted disclosure. Decision Replay
preserves sufficient governed evidence to reconstruct both the determination and the disclosure
decision."* Four layers: **Determination → Authority → Disclosure → Delivery.** It prevents:
collapsing policy provenance into an effective scalar; destroying audit truth to achieve suppression
silence; leaking protected information through HTTP status or reason text; replay that reconstructs
the decision but not why the consumer saw the response it saw. The planner names the criterion and
the sabotage that proves it.

**The pattern it was elevated from, his words:** *"we should not collapse internal
truth, governing authority, and permitted disclosure into the same representation. The fabric may
know why it refused, Policy determines what of that truth may cross the boundary, and the shell
represents the permitted external statement. Replay needs enough governed evidence to reconstruct
that transformation."*

### A. Policy-constraint layer and product defaults (B-07; first, foundational)
- **D-01: Provenance on every effective parameter, every time.** *(Amended, am.2.)* Each effective
  parameter records its authority — default, caller, policy, technical — and the identity/version of
  its source **where that source is a versioned artifact**, together with sufficient source
  provenance to reconstruct resolution otherwise. A caller value's provenance may be the request
  identity; a technical maximum is tied to backend/capability identity rather than a Control Plane
  artifact. Present even when nothing constrained the request. Replay pins it; the shell cites it.
  *"Provenance is part of the semantics, not merely audit metadata. Do not collapse authority after
  resolution."* *"Do not create fake artifacts just to satisfy a uniform provenance schema."* The
  ruling required provenance preserved, not artifact ids manufactured for authorities that do not
  naturally have them. The current
  `src/ccf/products/gates.py::effective_debt_ceiling` (`min(declared, requested)`) is the defect
  this rules out: it collapses two authorities into one number.
- **D-02: Product defaults live on the request contract** — beside `arguments` and
  `unsupported_arguments` on `src/ccf/products/request.py::ProductRequestContract` — and the SDK
  applies them before validation. *"They define what omission means for that product."* Today the
  SDK applies no defaults (`refuse_request` validates only); cp-04's `argument_defaults` /
  `argument_bounds` are example-local keys and migrate.
- **D-03: A policy constraint is a NEW Control Plane artifact class**, with its own reserved config
  section, registered and versioned like the others (`src/ccf/controlplane/`), carrying at least:
  policy id, policy version, applicability (product class, tenant, perspective, intent), parameter,
  operator, value, overridable, source authority, effective dates, reason/citation, priority.
  *"independent identity, version, effectivity and authority … should not disappear inside another
  bundle."* Not in product-class registration (`D-IMPL-251`).
- **D-04: Conflicts — most restrictive wins ONLY among constraints whose semantics are comparable;
  priority breaks ties only where restrictiveness is not comparable; priority must NEVER erase an
  applicable hard constraint.** Irreconcilable constraints (e.g. an empty `allowed_set`
  intersection) return a governed shell identifying a policy-resolution conflict, subject to
  disclosure.
- **D-05: v0 operator vocabulary:** `maximum`, `minimum`, `allowed_set`, `required`, `preferred`.
  `preferred` is routing guidance and can never cause a refusal (`D-IMPL-251`).
- **D-06: Effectivity is evaluated at the request's `asked_at`.** *"Otherwise replay of the same
  historical decision can resolve a different policy."* Replay pins the exact constraint artifact
  versions in force and the resolution outcome under the Policy Bindings Snapshot dimension.
- **D-07: "Overridable" requires an explicit override in the request (naming the policy) AND
  verified authority/entitlement of the caller/principal to exercise it.** *"Otherwise anyone who
  knows a policy ID could bypass it."* The override and its verification are recorded.
- **D-08: `PolicyConstraintResolver` is a Protocol with an in-process reference implementation**
  reading the registry, placed in the governed resolver path in the ruled sequence: apply defaults
  → overlay caller values → resolve policy constraints → validate the effective request → fulfilled
  response or shell refusal. Inputs: product class, request, principal/perspective, intent, tenant,
  `asked_at`.

### B. Debt and empty results (B-04, B-05, B-06; apply `D-IMPL-251`, do not reopen)
- **D-09: `ServeObligations.debt_ceiling` is RENAMED to a default tolerance.** It becomes the
  effective tolerance only when the caller omits one (worked case 2), with authority recorded as
  default. The declared-obligation gate's debt half (`_refuse_declared_obligations`,
  `obligation="debt-ceiling"`, the 409 route) is retired and replaced by the tolerance shell.
  *"Keeping debt_ceiling after changing its authority would perpetuate the conceptual error we just
  fixed."* Three debt concepts stay distinct: product default → informative; consumer-declared
  tolerance → enforce as requested; policy/regulatory constraint → enforce as governance.
- **D-10: One new refusal kind, `tolerance-exceeded`**, carrying the authority that established the
  tolerance (default / caller / policy), the observed debt, and the disclosable debt breakdown. A
  policy debt maximum arrives through the constraint layer on the `debt_tolerance` parameter and
  produces the same kind with authority policy. *"I would not make the shell itself imply that Debt
  is invalid; the product was constructible, but fulfillment was withheld because an applicable
  declared tolerance was exceeded."* `TOLERANCE_EXCEEDED` does not exist today (measured: 0 hits).
- **D-11: Computed-empty is SERVED, evidence-rich:** empty payload plus findings naming the
  operators that ran, the snapshot and plan they ran over, and a debt figure derived from that
  evidence. Zero Debt is legitimate when complete evidence establishes that zero is the answer.
  *"Zero results is not zero knowledge."* `_refuse_empty_results` (`gates.py:1052-1093`) stops
  refusing on cardinality; the declared half (`_refuse_empty_operators`, no declared/eligible
  operators = uncomputed) still refuses.
- **D-12: An operator that evaluates an EMPTY GRAPH did compute.** *(Amended, am.2 — the most
  important correction.)* Serve computed-empty and derive Context Debt from the evidence concerning
  completeness, coverage and source availability; **emptiness alone does not determine Debt.** If
  the authoritative universe is legitimately empty and that emptiness is well established, Debt can
  be zero; if the graph is empty because expected evidence is absent, ingestion failed or sources
  are unavailable, Debt can be high. An applicable tolerance may subsequently cause a shell
  response. *"Calling it uncomputed conflates absence of input with absence of execution."* *"Zero
  source entities is not automatically high Debt."* The cardinality → refusal shortcut this phase
  removes is NOT replaced by a cardinality → high-Debt shortcut: *"Empty cardinality cannot itself
  become a proxy for Context Debt any more than it could remain a proxy for refusal."* hw1's
  negative test (`tests/examples/` beside `test_hw1_two_transports.py`) is re-derived to assert
  served-empty with evidence-derived Debt, and the tolerance shell only where that Debt exceeds an
  applicable tolerance — not `empty-operator-result`; the two 24.5 SDK tests on `L-24`'s returned
  half are re-derived (`D-IMPL-242` am.1).

### C. The refusal shell and replay (B-02, B-08, LC-01)
- **D-13: Every refusal mints a full five-dimension Decision Replay record.** *"Refusal is itself a
  decision and deserves the same five-dimensional replay model. Do not create second-class
  replay."* `src/ccf/replay/record.py::build_replay_record` must accept a refusal outcome with NO
  product (today it requires `product` and takes a refusal only as a pre-serialized string via
  `mint_assembly_refusal`); the fabric snapshot dimension carries the pre-assembly lineage or
  states there is none.
- **D-14: `RefusalEnvelope` is extended ADDITIVELY** (`src/ccf/products/refusal.py:153-217`,
  `extra=forbid` preserved): policy bindings in force (identity/version), a replay reference,
  permitted evidence and lineage, and the D-01 provenance record. Already present: request
  identity, product id/version, `asked_at` (as `temporal_boundary`), reason code. No wrapper type.
- **D-15: The identity refusal under C4 — convert GOVERNED DETERMINATIONS, not
  configuration/invariant failures.** Tenant, role, jurisdiction and membership outcomes the fabric
  can governedly determine become returned refusal reasons on the existing kind
  (`AssemblyRefusal`, new reasons such as tenant mismatch / undeclared role); `24.5-L-11`
  superseded; `AssemblyRefused`'s docstring boundary rewritten. *"Don't mechanically convert every
  existing AssemblyRefused call merely because it currently uses that class."* *(Amended, am.2.)*
  **The classification rule is mechanical and is Dave's, not the planner's:** *"If the fabric has
  enough information to reach a valid governed determination, return data. If it cannot reliably
  reach a governed determination because an internal invariant/configuration/runtime condition has
  failed, raise."* The planner inventories and classifies each of the 28 raise sites (2 in
  `src/ccf/agents/assembler.py:435,443`; 26 in examples, incl. cp-04 `deployment.py` :473, :1847,
  :1853, :2046-2069, :2193) against that rule, with the reason recorded per site. **Any site that
  cannot be unambiguously classified returns to Dave for a ruling before criteria are locked or
  implementation begins** — the exception boundary is not reinterpreted during implementation.
- **D-16: Assembler identity and version enter replay under the Perspective Conditioning
  dimension** (CLAUDE.md §6.2 places agent identity/version there), as new keyword-only inputs to
  `build_replay_record` (`LC-01`, `D-IMPL-245` am.1). Today: 0 hits for `assembler_id` /
  `assembler_version` under `src/ccf/replay/`.
- **D-17: Disclosure is policy-controlled, minimal and suppression-safe by default.** A disclosure
  level on the policy regime decides whether reason detail, ambiguity candidates and the debt
  breakdown appear in the shell.

### D. The two doors (B-02, B-03)
- **D-18: HTTP — class-based statuses with a UNIFORM body; NO known governed refusal may become
  500.** The 24.5 rulings stand (`declared-obligation-refusal` → 409, `product-request-violation` →
  400; `runtime/ccf_runtime/http_api/routes/product.py::retrieve`). Every remaining kind in
  `REFUSAL_KINDS` gets a status by class; do NOT hard-code all entitlement/fulfillment to 409 —
  authorization semantics may warrant 403 internally while suppression-safe policy deliberately maps
  externally to a less revealing class. *"The response body and disclosure policy must be
  authoritative, not HTTP status."* 500 remains only for a kind absent from `REFUSAL_KINDS` (an
  invariant violation = true failure).
- **D-19: The MCP standing resource's body becomes the shell** (canonical bytes carrying the kind)
  when the resolver refuses — `runtime/ccf_runtime/mcp/egress.py::context_product` (:813-865) has
  no channel today. *"Not-found would destroy the distinction and replayability."*
- **D-20: No governed determination reaches an MCP client as a tool error.** Entitlement denial
  (`ProductEntitlementRefused`, raised at `egress.py:1245-1252`) becomes a shell, suppression-safe.
  `ToolError` is reserved for CCF/runtime failure and carries an opaque failure id.

## Specific Ideas

**The four worked cases (Dave, 2026-09-04) — the design starts here, not at classes:**

```
Product default debt tolerance: .25
Caller:                         .30
Policy:                         none
→ effective caller tolerance .30            (authority: caller)

Product default:                .25
Caller:                         omitted
Policy:                         none
→ effective tolerance .25                   (authority: default)

Product default:                .25
Caller:                         .30
Policy maximum:                 .20
→ policy boundary .20                       (authority: policy)

Product default depth:             6
Caller requests:                   8
Technical maximum:               100
Policy:                          none
→ depth 8 is valid                          (bound: technical)
```

*"I would avoid describing the first three simply as values that are always collapsed into one
'effective tolerance.' We need to preserve provenance of authority. Otherwise we recreate exactly
the defect the scout found with min(). The resolved request should know why a bound exists —
default, caller constraint, policy constraint, or technical capability — because that determines
both the response semantics and what replay must record."*

**Three representations, never one:** internal truth (why the fabric refused) → governing
authority (what Policy permits to cross) → permitted disclosure (the shell). Replay retains enough
to reconstruct the transformation.

**Refusal vocabulary for the constraint layer:** `preferred` is guidance; `maximum` is policy; only
the latter can cause a shell refusal (`D-IMPL-251`).
