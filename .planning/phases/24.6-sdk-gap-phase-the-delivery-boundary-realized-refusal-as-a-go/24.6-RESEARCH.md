#### A. Policy-constraint layer and product defaults (B-07; first, foundational)
- **D-01: Provenance on every effective parameter, every time.** *(Amended, am.2.)* Each effective parameter records its authority — default, caller, policy, technical — and the identity/version of its source **where that source is a versioned artifact**, together with sufficient source provenance to reconstruct resolution otherwise. A caller value's provenance may be the request identity; a technical maximum is tied to backend/capability identity rather than a Control Plane artifact. Present even when nothing constrained the request. Replay pins it; the shell cites it. *"Provenance is part of the semantics, not merely audit metadata. Do not collapse authority after resolution."* *"Do not create fake artifacts just to satisfy a uniform provenance schema."* The current `src/ccf/products/gates.py::effective_debt_ceiling` (`min(declared, requested)`) is the defect this rules out: it collapses two authorities into one number.
- **D-02: Product defaults live on the request contract** — beside `arguments` and `unsupported_arguments` on `src/ccf/products/request.py::ProductRequestContract` — and the SDK applies them before validation. *"They define what omission means for that product."* Today the SDK applies no defaults (`refuse_request` validates only); cp-04's `argument_defaults` / `argument_bounds` are example-local keys and migrate.
- **D-03: A policy constraint is a NEW Control Plane artifact class**, with its own reserved config section, registered and versioned like the others (`src/ccf/controlplane/`), carrying at least: policy id, policy version, applicability (product class, tenant, perspective, intent), parameter, operator, value, overridable, source authority, effective dates, reason/citation, priority. *"independent identity, version, effectivity and authority … should not disappear inside another bundle."* Not in product-class registration (`D-IMPL-251`).
- **D-04: Conflicts — most restrictive wins ONLY among constraints whose semantics are comparable; priority breaks ties only where restrictiveness is not comparable; priority must NEVER erase an applicable hard constraint.** Irreconcilable constraints (e.g. an empty `allowed_set` intersection) return a governed shell identifying a policy-resolution conflict, subject to disclosure.
- **D-05: v0 operator vocabulary:** `maximum`, `minimum`, `allowed_set`, `required`, `preferred`. `preferred` is routing guidance and can never cause a refusal (`D-IMPL-251`).
- **D-06: Effectivity is evaluated at the request's `asked_at`.** *"Otherwise replay of the same historical decision can resolve a different policy."* Replay pins the exact constraint artifact versions in force and the resolution outcome under the Policy Bindings Snapshot dimension.
- **D-07: "Overridable" requires an explicit override in the request (naming the policy) AND verified authority/entitlement of the caller/principal to exercise it.** *"Otherwise anyone who knows a policy ID could bypass it."* The override and its verification are recorded.
- **D-08: `PolicyConstraintResolver` is a Protocol with an in-process reference implementation** reading the registry, placed in the governed resolver path in the ruled sequence: apply defaults → overlay caller values → resolve policy constraints → validate the effective request → fulfilled response or shell refusal. Inputs: product class, request, principal/perspective, intent, tenant, `asked_at`.

## M5 — Determinism evidence: what makes "same request + same governed state + same `asked_at` → same …" true today

**For a FULFILLED product, true by construction, and asserted:**

## Architecture Patterns

### The resolved-request value (D-01, D-08) — the pattern the worked cases force
The four worked cases in `<specifics>` all read the same way: a parameter's effective value is a triple, not a number. Case 1 (`.25` default, `.30` caller, no policy) → `.30`, **caller**. Case 2 (caller omitted) → `.25`, **default**. Case 3 (policy maximum `.20`) → `.20`, **policy**, and the caller's `.30` is *still recorded as the caller's request* so the shell can say the boundary was policy's, not that the caller asked for `.20`. Case 4 (default 6, caller 8, technical 100) → `8`, **caller**, bounded by **technical** — two authorities present at once (the value's, and the bound's). So the resolution output per parameter is at least `(effective_value, authority ∈ {default, caller, policy, technical}, source)` where `source` is a versioned-artifact pin for `default` (the request contract, key+version) and `policy` (the constraint artifact, policy id+version), the request identity for `caller`, and a backend/capability identity for `technical` — never a manufactured artifact id (am.2 (1)). Comparability (D-04): `maximum`/`minimum` compare on the same numeric axis; `allowed_set` intersects; `required` and `preferred` are not restrictiveness at all — `preferred` can never produce a shell (D-05). A precedence table that collapses to a scalar is the `min()` defect in new clothes.

### The gate step after D-09/D-10
Replace `debt_tolerance = effective_debt_ceiling(...)` (`resolver.py:746-749`) and the debt half of `_refuse_declared_obligations` (`gates.py:481-514`) with one comparison of `candidate.debt.debt` against the resolved `debt_tolerance` triple; over → `ToleranceExceededRefusal(gate=<token>, authority, tolerance, observed, disclosable breakdown)` (a `GateRefusal` subclass keeps the `ServableProduct | GateRefusal` union and the census at `test_structural_gates.py:757` honest). The lineage half stays. `DeclaredObligationRefusal.obligation` loses `"debt-ceiling"` from its `Literal` (`gates.py:376`).

### Computed-empty (D-11, D-12)
`_refuse_empty_results` stops returning a refusal; `execution.empty_results` becomes **findings** on the served product (operators that returned empty, the plan id, `store_snapshot_pin`, `executed_queries` hashes — all already on `ExecutionResult`, `engine.py:212-245`). The scorer's zero-observation branch (`debt_scorer.py:281-290`) must be re-derived from evidence: completeness from the consultation/coverage record (`debt.py:253` is `1 − available/required`, the right shape), freshness from source refresh recency (`contract.py:822`), source availability from `source_attestation`. ### The refusal record (D-13, D-16)
`build_replay_record` gains a refusal branch: `execution` optional (a pre-assembly refusal has none → dimension 1 states "no snapshot" positively, as every sentinel in that module does — `record.py:141-195`); `product` optional; a new keyword carrying the shell; `assembler_id`/`assembler_version` as keyword-only inputs landing on `PerspectiveConditioning` with sentinel defaults marked hash-transparent (`D-IMPL-243`). `_admissible_refusal_block` (`:758-786`) widens from "a `ProductRequestViolation` round-trip" to "any `REFUSAL_KINDS` member's envelope", re-canonicalized. The resolver mints the record on both refusal branches (`:693`, `:765`) *before* returning, and `serve`'s ordering discipline (record first, delivery second) applies to the shell too.

### Disclosure (D-17, D-27) at the doors (D-18..D-20)
One function, in `src/`, computes the *permitted statement* from `(internal refusal, disclosure level)`; both doors render only its output. The HTTP status is looked up from the *permitted* kind (so an authorization outcome mapped to a less revealing class externally does so by policy, not by the door), the body is one shape for every kind (retire the `refusal_detail` body for entitlement at `product.py:449-457` in favor of the envelope shape, or vice versa — one, not two), and 500 is reachable only from a kind absent from `REFUSAL_KINDS`.
