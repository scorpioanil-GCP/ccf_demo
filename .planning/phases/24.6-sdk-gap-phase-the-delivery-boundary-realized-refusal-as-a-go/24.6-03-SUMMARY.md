# Phase 24.6 Plan 03: the resolver seam — effectivity, the verified override, and the technical capability Summary

The Authority layer stopped being a library and became the path: the Control Plane is
now read at the request's own instant, an override is honored only where a named policy
and a declared entitlement agree, the backend can be asked what it can technically do,
and what was resolved is pinned into replay dimension 2 beside the exact versions it was
resolved from. `effective_debt_ceiling` is gone.

## What was built

**Task 1 — the seam and its slots** (commit `f1b4c85d`).

`constraints_in_force(control_plane, *, asked_at)` walks `keys_of_class` →
`versions` → **unaudited** `peek` → exact `resolve`. Two properties are structural
rather than documented: the read surface it takes (`_ConstraintSource`, module-private)
**has no "latest" method at all**, so reaching for one is not a discipline but an
impossibility — the `ReplayResolver` argument applied to a function; and the effectivity
window is read by one private `_in_window`, called from both this walk and
`applicable(...)`, so the fabric cannot answer *"which policy was in force?"* two ways
at two layers.

`ConstraintEffectivityConflict` raises when two authoritative statements are in force at
one instant. `PolicyConstraintResolver` is the Protocol, with `resolver_id` and
`resolver_version` on the surface for `DebtFactorScorerProtocol`'s reason;
`ReferencePolicyConstraintResolver` is the frozen-dataclass reference, and it is the
only thing here that performs I/O — it fetches, then calls `24.6-02`'s pure function.

Three slots joined their contracts, each hash-transparent at its sentinel:
`ProductRequest.policy_overrides` (a request carrying an override is a **different
question**, so it is content-addressed with the rest of it),
`ProductRequestContract.capability_bindings` (which declared parameter a capability
token bounds — the token, never the number), and
`ProductEntitlementGrant.overridable_policies` (which exceptions a principal may
exercise, and **nothing else**: it grants no class, no role and no subject).

`TraversalDepthCapability` is the graph Protocol's structural addition. Fluree and Neo4j
answer it with their own `MAX_DEPTH_CEILING` and `graph_identity`; SQLite implements
neither member and gains no stub. **The graph seam gained no refusal path** — an
over-ceiling depth there still declines the native translation and is answered by the
fallback; what changed is that a layer with a governed response can now *ask*.

**Task 2 — the ruled sequence, the deletion, and the pin** (commit `03d056ff`).

`GovernedResolver.__call__` now resolves before it reads: defaults applied, caller
values overlaid, the constraints in force at `asked_at` resolved with provenance, the
capabilities held against them, and only then the **effective** request checked. The
effective request is a *sibling*, never a replacement — `RequestIdentity.parameters`
and replay dimension 5 both keep what the caller supplied, which is the entire reason a
refusal can still say *"you asked for .30 and policy `a` bounds you to .20"*.

`effective_debt_ceiling` and its dead helper `_as_finite_decimal` are deleted.
`PolicyBindingsSnapshot.request_resolution` carries the `EffectiveRequest` rendered from
the **value** by the builder (a caller cannot hand over a pre-serialized block, so one
resolution cannot land as a family of blocks), and every applied artifact pin — the
constraints, the request contract, the entitlement policy when an override was verified
— joins `bindings`. Sources with no version lane are deliberately *not* bindings:
minting a lane for a backend capability is the fake artifact amendment 2 (1) forbids, so
they travel whole inside the resolution instead.

**Task 3 — the effectivity and override tests, with their sabotage** (commit
`71702e6c`). `tests/products/test_policy_constraint_resolver.py`: 19 tests, 956 lines.
Two constraint keys over adjacent half-open windows; the boundary instant; the pins as
content addresses; `resolve_latest` monkeypatched to raise (with its own control proving
the guard can fire); the double-effectivity conflict and its "superseded is not a
conflict" control; the override four ways; and three tests through the booted resolver.

## Deviations from Plan

**1. [Rule 2 — governance] The gate's debt half keeps the non-loosening fold as the
INTERIM, instead of the plan's literal replacement line.**
The plan says to replace the `effective_debt_ceiling` call with
`ceiling = debt_tolerance if debt_tolerance is not None else obligations.debt_ceiling`.
Measured before deciding: that line makes a caller-supplied tolerance the ceiling in
**both** directions, and two shipped assertions go RED —
`test_a_loosening_request_is_refused_by_name_the_class_ceiling_stands` (a request asking
0.9 against a class ceiling of 0.5 must not admit a `D_c` of 0.7) and
`test_an_undeclared_class_serves_at_any_debt` (a request cannot CREATE a ceiling). Both
are `24.5-L-14`'s own governance, and deleting them to admit this change is the defect
`ccf.products.resolver`'s own module docstring names as this phase's signature. So the
deleted helper's two limbs are inlined at the call site, verbatim in behavior, under a
comment naming the interim and `24.6-04`. `C-06` is unaffected: the function is gone,
the grep is 0 in `src` and `runtime`, and the fold that *establishes* the effective
tolerance is the Authority layer's. See F-2 for the honesty gap this leaves for one wave.

**2. `_as_finite_decimal` was deleted too.**
Not named by the plan. It existed only to coerce `effective_debt_ceiling`'s `requested:
object`; with that gone it had zero callers in `src`, `runtime` and `tests`. Dead private
code that nothing can reach is not an interim, it is a defect.

**3. The Control Plane read surface is a module-PRIVATE Protocol.**
`ControlPlaneResolution` (the shipped read-only Protocol) has `resolve`, `versions`,
`peek` and `artifact_class_of` but **not** `keys_of_class`, and discovering which keys
hold constraints is exactly what this seam does. A public `ConstraintSource` would have
been an eighth public name the plan did not fix — plan 01's deviation 3, same
measurement — so `_ConstraintSource` is private, and `build_governed_resolver` checks
conformance with `isinstance` and raises a `TypeError` naming both ways to fix a Control
Plane that cannot be asked. `ControlPlaneProtocol` was **not** widened: that would be a
FABR-07 remote-plane obligation and a `D-IMPL-82`-class ruling.

**4. `examples/hw1-companies-over-revenue/README.md` was edited — one line.**

minted** replay record, which is correct: the record now says more. hw1's README fence
pins one (`…@762d8cec` → `…@8b68efaf`), and
`test_the_hw1_readme_fence_is_the_in_process_commands_output` compares the fence to a

**F-2. For one wave, the record can say a tolerance the gate did not enforce.** With the
interim (deviation 1), a caller asking for a tolerance *looser* than the class's ceiling
has that value recorded in dimension 2 with authority `caller`, while the gate holds the
product to the class's number. Both facts are true and both are recorded, but they are
not the same number. This closes in `24.6-04`, where the class's declared ceiling becomes
an authority *in the resolution* and there is one fold rather than two. Flagging it
because it is exactly the kind of gap that reads as a defect to a reviewer who finds it
without this note.

**F-3. The entitlement policy's version in force is a reading, not a ruling.** The plan
says the reference reads the tenant's `ProductEntitlementPolicy` *"at the pinned version
the deployment put in force"*, but supplies no pin on this seam — the resolver holds a
key and nothing else, and the artifact carries no effectivity window. I ruled: exactly
one registered version is the one in force; **zero** versions (or no key) entitles no
override, which is deny-by-default and matches the delivery door's posture; and **two or
more** raises `ConstraintEffectivityConflict`, because honoring an override under an
undetermined grant is the elevation Q7 forbids. Registration is content-addressed and
idempotent, so a deployment registering the same policy repeatedly still has one version.
The alternative — take the most recently registered — is the "latest" read this whole
plan exists to make unreachable. Stated because it is a reading.

**F-4. A resolution rehydrated from the record is not type-identical to the one that was
written.** `EffectiveParameter.value` is typed `Any`, and the canonical form renders a
`Decimal` as a quoted number (a float is refused outright as not bit-stable), so
`EffectiveRequest.model_validate_json(request_resolution)` returns `'0.20'` where the
original held `Decimal("0.20")`. **The record is faithful** — the bytes are what replay
compares, and they round-trip exactly — but a consumer rehydrating the block gets a
string. The tests read it as a decimal explicitly and say why. If you want type fidelity
on rehydration, that is a typed-value decision on `provenance.py`, not a fix here.

**F-5. The capability derivation reaches nothing on any shipped boot path, by
construction.** `build_governed_resolver` derives `capabilities` structurally from
`fabric.handle` and its `.reader` — but `FabricHandle` exposes a *reader*, and the two
ceilinged backends declare their bound on the *store*, which the handle deliberately does
not carry. So today it derives the empty tuple everywhere, which is the honest answer
(nothing the resolver is handed can report a ceiling), and a deployment that holds its
store passes `capabilities` explicitly. The alternative — putting the ceiling on the
reader, or exposing the store on the handle — is a graph-seam change nobody ruled, so I
did not make it. The Protocol, the backends' answers and the resolver's reading of them
are all in place and tested; only the *automatic* wiring is empty.

reads `c3224bb4…`"*. `CRITERIA-LOCK.md` Amendment 2 (`D-IMPL-256`) added criterion 36 and
re-pinned to `014dcd64…`. **The digest on this tree reproduces `014dcd64…` exactly**, with
