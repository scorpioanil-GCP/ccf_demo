## 1. Selection of areas and priority order (2026-09-04, before the questions)

> I would select all four SDK seams on page 1. They are not competing alternatives; together they define the 24.6 boundary work we just agreed to.
>
> In priority order, I would discuss them as follows:
>
> 1. The policy-constraint layer — first. This is foundational because it establishes the authority model we just defined: policy boundary > caller value > product default. We need to settle the artifact model, constraint operators, applicability/effectivity, conflict resolution, and what gets pinned into replay. Until this is clear, we cannot cleanly fix the debt behavior.
> 2. Debt and empty results — second. Apply D-IMPL-251 rather than reopen it. The current min(class ceiling, caller tolerance) implementation is wrong because it collapses two different authorities. Product-class debt becomes a default; caller tolerance remains an enforceable consumer constraint; actual policy limits come from the new policy layer. And preserve the distinction we just made: uncomputed → shell refusal; computed-empty → served product, with Debt based on evidence rather than cardinality.
> 3. The refusal shell and replay — third. The scout found a substantive gap. A refusal is a governed response, so it needs enough identity to be independently understood and replayed. I would expect 24.6 to establish that a refusal carries or references at least request/product identity, asked_at, applicable policy identity/version, governed reason, permitted evidence/lineage, and a replay identity. Most importantly, Decision Replay must be able to record a request that produced no fulfilled product.
> 4. HTTP and MCP doors — fourth. Once the response semantics are settled, make the doors faithfully transport them. The current ~18-kind → opaque 500 fall-through directly violates D-IMPL-251. Likewise, MCP resource access cannot simply lack a refusal channel. The rule should be transport-independent:
>
> Governed determination → governed response. Technical/runtime failure → transport error.
>
> I would not choose Other on this page unless Claude needs it to capture one additional issue: product defaults themselves need an SDK representation/resolution mechanism. The scout says the SDK validates requests but applies no defaults. That's adjacent to policy constraints but conceptually distinct. I would explicitly bring it into the policy/default discussion rather than create a fifth workstream.
>
> There is also one design caution I would give Claude before the discussion starts: don't begin with Pydantic classes. Start with the authority/resolution semantics and a few worked cases. For example:
>
> ```
> Product default debt tolerance: .25
> Caller:                         .30
> Policy:                         none
> → effective caller tolerance .30
> Product default:                .25
> Caller:                         omitted
> Policy:                         none
> → effective tolerance .25
> Product default:                .25
> Caller:                         .30
> Policy maximum:                 .20
> → policy boundary .20
> Product default depth:             6
> Caller requests:                   8
> Technical maximum:               100
> Policy:                          none
> → depth 8 is valid
> ```
>
> One subtlety: I would avoid describing the first three simply as values that are always collapsed into one "effective tolerance." We need to preserve provenance of authority. Otherwise we recreate exactly the defect the scout found with min(). The resolved request should know why a bound exists—default, caller constraint, policy constraint, or technical capability—because that determines both the response semantics and what replay must record.
>
> So on the screen you have now, I would check the first four boxes and proceed. The policy-constraint layer should be the first substantive design discussion.
>
> For the second screen, select both:
>
> 1. cp-04 depth and republish
> 2. Ledger fence and the small carries
>
> I would not select Other unless Claude surfaces another issue during discussion.
>
> The first is mandatory because D-IMPL-251 C5 explicitly superseded cp-04's depth semantics. We need to decide the proper SDK representation of the default versus the genuine technical limit, what the product exposes as requested/actual depth and cost, and then rebuild/republish cp-04 only after that SDK behavior is settled.
>
> The second is equally important because 24.6 should consume the entire B-01…B-14 carry roster deliberately. We don't want another phase where a seemingly minor stale config claim, gate count, suppression behavior, or process item survives simply because it fell outside the denominator.
>
> So across the two screens my selections would be:

## 3. The invariant, elevated (2026-09-04, after the record was read back)

> Yes — that captures my rulings exactly.
>
> The last paragraph is especially important because it gives 24.6 a deeper organizing principle than simply implementing 28 individual decisions. I would elevate it into an explicit architectural invariant:
>
> Internal truth, governing authority, and permitted disclosure are distinct concerns. The fabric preserves the governed truth of why a determination was reached; Policy determines what of that truth may cross the consumer boundary; the response shell expresses only the permitted disclosure. Decision Replay preserves sufficient governed evidence to reconstruct both the determination and the disclosure decision.
>
> That gives us four distinct layers:
>
> Determination → Authority → Disclosure → Delivery
>
> and prevents several future mistakes at once: collapsing policy provenance into an effective scalar, destroying audit truth to achieve suppression silence, leaking protected information through HTTP status/reason text, or producing replay that can reconstruct the decision but not why the consumer saw the particular response it saw.
>

> I reviewed both files closely. Overall, 24.6 is scoped correctly. >

>
> **1. D-01 is slightly over-specified**
>
> The context currently says:
>
> "Each effective value is recorded with its authority … and the artifact id and version that supplied it, present even when nothing constrained the request."
>
> The principle is right, but product defaults, caller values and technical capabilities may not all come from versioned "artifacts." The ruling itself said provenance must be preserved; it did not require manufacturing artifact IDs for authorities that don't naturally have them.
>
> I would change this to:
>
> Each effective parameter records its authority (default, caller, policy, technical) and the identity/version of its source where that source is a versioned artifact, together with sufficient source provenance to reconstruct resolution otherwise.
>
> For example, a caller value's provenance may be the request identity, while a technical maximum might be tied to backend/capability identity rather than a Control Plane artifact.
>
> Do not create fake artifacts just to satisfy a uniform provenance schema.
>
> **2. D-12 makes an architectural conclusion about Debt that we did not quite rule**
>
> This is the biggest issue I see.
>
> The context says:
>
> "Served computed-empty with the empty snapshot as evidence of deficiency, hence high evidence-derived Debt…"
>
> I agreed that an empty graph was computed-empty, but "empty graph ⇒ high Debt" does not necessarily follow.
>
> We just established:
>
> Zero results is not zero knowledge.
>
> The symmetric point is:
>
> Zero source entities is not automatically high Debt.
>
> If the authoritative universe is legitimately empty and that emptiness is well established, Debt could be zero. If the graph is empty because expected evidence is absent, ingestion failed, sources are unavailable, etc., Debt could be high.
>
> So I would amend D-12 to:
>
> An operator evaluating an empty graph has computed. Serve computed-empty and derive Context Debt from the evidence concerning completeness, coverage and source availability; emptiness alone does not determine Debt. An applicable tolerance may subsequently cause a shell response.
>
> This is important because otherwise 24.6 would remove the cardinality → refusal shortcut only to replace it with a cardinality → high Debt shortcut.
>
> **3. D-15 gives the planner too much architectural discretion**
>
> The context currently puts under Claude's discretion:
>
> "Which of the 28 raise AssemblyRefused sites are governed determinations … the planner's classification…"
>
> I would narrow that.
>
> The planner absolutely should inventory and classify the 28 sites. But whether a genuinely ambiguous case belongs on the governed-response side or technical-failure side is an architectural decision, not planning discretion.
>
> The rule should be mechanical:
>
> If the fabric has enough information to reach a valid governed determination, return data. If it cannot reliably reach a governed determination because an internal invariant/configuration/runtime condition has failed, raise.
>
> Have the planner classify every site against that rule. Any site that cannot be unambiguously classified comes back to you before criteria are locked or implementation begins.
>
> That protects the new exception boundary from gradually being reinterpreted during implementation.

>

> * presence/absence of evidence fields,
> * lineage,
> * replay reference behavior,
> * message text,

>

>
> Unauthorized and nonexistent subjects must be consumer-observationally equivalent at the same disclosure level: same transport status, kind, external reason, body schema/field presence and permitted disclosure behavior. Internal truth/replay may differ according to policy.

> I would not require byte identity, because request identity, timestamps, replay IDs, etc. may legitimately differ. The criterion should test semantic/observable equivalence, not accidental byte equality.
