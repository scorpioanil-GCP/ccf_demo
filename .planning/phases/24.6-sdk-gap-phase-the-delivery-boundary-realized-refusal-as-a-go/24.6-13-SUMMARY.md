# Phase 24.6 Plan 13: cp-04 on the delivery boundary — the bound of 6 deleted, depth 8 served, the technical limit refused by name Summary

Dave, `D-IMPL-253` Q21, verbatim: **"6 is measurement/default history; 100 is
the genuine technical capability boundary."**

One sentence, and it separates two things cp-04 had been holding as one number
under one key. How deep this estate *goes* is a measurement. Which depths a
caller may *ask* is a contract. They were both `argument_bounds.max_depth.maximum`,
so a request at depth 8 was refused for exhibiting nothing rather than for
exceeding anything. It is served now, and what the walk actually reached
travels in the payload beside what was asked.

`MAX_DEPTH_CEILING`, imported; the identity is `fluree/<ledger>`,
credential-free and never the URL.

## Deviations from Plan

**1. [Rule 3 — blocking] The plan's premise "the Fluree store cp-04 opens" does
not exist in the tree.** This is first because it changed how the whole
capability seam is shaped.

store the deployment opens"* and to locate it with
`grep -n "FlureeGraphStore\|fluree" deployment.py`. Measured: cp-04 opens no

`/v1/fluree/query` (`deployment.py::graph_source` /

the SDK store to ask it the question would have **created a ledger** —

technical capability (D-21) is surfaced from a backend to the resolver (a
Protocol property vs a registered capability artifact) — either is acceptable

code"*). `GraphLegCapability` conforms structurally, its number is the SDK
adapter's own constant imported rather than restated, and its identity is
credential-free. Both conditions hold, measured.

statements, `test:` references, `sabotage:` lines and `observed_red` blocks were

## Findings for Dave

**F-1. cp-04's gate roster keys its obligations on a criterion number that is
not unique across phases, and 24.6 is where it collides.**

`artifacts/gates.yaml` gives each gate a bare integer `criterion`, and
`test_the_criteria_map_one_to_one_onto_the_gates` requires those integers to be
distinct. The latent ambiguity is already visible in the tree: `CFT-G18` claims
`criterion: 19`, which is Phase **24.5.1**'s numbering, held apart from 24.4's
25–40 by a constant called `RETURN_TRIP_CRITERIA` — and held apart successfully
only because 19 is outside 25–40 by luck.

Phase 24.6's criterion 33 is D-23's (depth and cost in the findings). Phase
24.4's criterion 33 is `CFT-G13`'s. They are two different obligations with one
key, and the roster cannot hold both.

Three ways out, and I took none of them:

1. **Leave it.** D-23's claim ships as a test with both sabotages watched RED,
   and the served-deep half is inside `CFT-G03`'s statement. What is lost is
   the roster's own record that the obligation exists — which is what
   `check_product_gates.py` reads. Zero further work; this is what shipped.
2. **A `criterion_phase` key** on the gate row, with the map keyed on the pair.
   Honest, small in lines, and it makes an ambiguity that is already there
   explicit — but it widens the CLOSED schema
   `docs/schemas/product-gates/cp.product-gates.json`, which every `cp-*` rung
   validates against, so it is a change to five rosters' contract rather than
   to cp-04's.
3. **Renumber**, which would mean the roster claiming a criterion the phase did
   not lock. Refused outright.

My reading is that (2) is right and that it belongs to whichever phase next
touches the roster contract, not to a plan fenced on cp-04's behavior. It bites
again the moment any future phase's criterion number falls in 25–40.

**F-2. The shared estate already carries fourteen `cp04-*` branches from prior
runs, so the standing "do not seed the shared estate" instruction may no longer
describe this project's practice.**

The instruction I was given is explicit and I followed it: `make leg-fluree` and
