# Phase 24.6 Plan 08: identity determinations return, and disclosure is one step Summary

Dave, on the exception boundary (`D-IMPL-253` amendment 2 (3), verbatim):
**"If the fabric has enough information to reach a valid governed
determination, return data. If it cannot reliably reach a governed
determination because an internal invariant/configuration/runtime condition has
failed, raise."**

the **Determination** now returns what it can determine; **Disclosure** exists
at all, as one function; and the delivery-boundary **Authority** step mints and
discloses before it returns, so a transport has nothing left to decide.

## What was built

**Task 1 — the two `src/` determinations return; `AssemblyRefused` narrowed**
(commit `4e42a260`).

`AssemblyRefused` was not deleted and nothing converts it. Its **meaning**
narrowed to what Dave's rule leaves it: an internal invariant, a configuration
contradiction, a runtime condition. `24.5-L-11`'s *"kept as a raise, and not
retyped"* framing is named on the class as superseded, and the second half of
Q15 — *"Don't mechanically convert every existing AssemblyRefused call merely
because it currently uses that class"* — has its own control test, because
without it the two conversions read as *every* raise becoming data.

The SDK's own assembler returns both determinations, carrying two new SDK
tokens. Each raise's prose is carried unchanged as `detail`: the channel
changed and the statement did not.

`ContextAssemblerProtocol.assemble`'s union is now the roster of
`REFUSAL_KINDS` members reachable at that seam, `AnchorAmbiguityRefusal`
included — which LC-02 measured as returnable there and absent from the
annotation since 24.5.

`SubjectUnavailableRefusal` is the ONE canonical outcome a nonexistent subject
and an unauthorized principal share. **It has no `subject` field**, and that is
mechanism rather than discipline: naming the subject is itself the leak, and a
field a disclosure step had to remember to blank is a field that will one day
cross.

**Task 2 — the Disclosure layer** (commit `b52f996e`).

`disclose(envelope, *, level, policy) -> tuple[RefusalEnvelope,
DisclosureDecision]` is the single step from the internal determination to the
shell. Measured, not asserted:

```
disclose( called in src/ccf                       → disclosure.py (the def) + resolver.py (1 call)
RefusalEnvelope.disclosure written in src/ccf     → disclosure.py only
"disclose_only" anywhere in src/ or runtime/      → 0
```

Three levels, `minimal` the safe default, held on the tenant's entitlement
policy with a per-principal override on the grant. **A withheld value is
replaced and its key is kept**, so the shell's *shape* is identical for every
internal cause at one level — amendment 2 (4) names field presence explicitly,
and a shape channel is harder to notice than a prose one.

The recorder now records the **internal** envelope together with the
`DisclosureDecision`, and returns the **disclosed** shell with its address. That
ordering is the whole architecture: replay reconstructs what was determined
*and* why the consumer saw what it saw.

**Task 3 — the governed delivery-boundary step** (commit `abd94f02`).

`govern_entitlement` is the one step every entitling boundary calls. It reads
the determination instant **once** from the deployment's declared clock, runs
the shared resolution order, and on a denial mints and discloses before
returning. A caller gets the entitled `Perspective` or an already-recorded,
already-disclosed shell.

```
except ProductEntitlementRefused under runtime/ccf_runtime/authz/  → 1  (was 0)
wall-clock calls in delivery_boundary.py (AST)                     → 0
`.clock()` calls in delivery_boundary.py (AST)                     → 1, on the entitlement source
transport tokens named in delivery_boundary.py                     → 0
```

`recorded_boundary` is the construction-time check, and the posture is written
on `UnrecordableDeliveryBoundaryError` and nowhere else, so the three builders
(plans 09/10/16) cannot drift into three explanations of one rule.

## Deviations from Plan

**1. [Rule 4 — architectural; the one thing in this row that is arguably Dave's]
The plan's absent-policy pin is unbuildable, so the absence is recorded where it
IS representable and `policy` is `None`.** This is first because it is the only
acceptance criterion in the plan I did not discharge as written.

The plan's interfaces block says the deciding pin for a **measured absence**
(`ERROR_PRODUCT_POLICY_ABSENT`) is *"the same identity with `version=None` — the
key that was consulted and resolved nothing, a positive claim in the existing
shape."* Measured before deciding: it is **not** representable in the existing
shape. `ProvenanceSource`'s shipped `model_validator` refuses exactly that:

```
an 'artifact' provenance source must carry its version: replay resolves an
exact version and never 'latest' (D-IMPL-26)
```

and `PolicyBinding.version` is a required `str`, so the dimension-2 binding form
refuses it too. The three ways out were all bad in different ways:

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| widen the validator to admit `version=None` on `artifact` | weakens a shipped structural guard to admit this row's change — the defect plan 03's deviation 1 and plan 07's deviation 1 each refused |
| add a fourth `kind` to the closed `Literal` | a surface widening in `provenance.py`, a module outside this plan's `files_modified`, on a vocabulary `D-IMPL-253` amendment 2 (1) shaped |
| pin `version=""` (what the audit event records for the absence) | a **false** lane: an examiner resolving version `""` gets nothing while the record claims an artifact governed at a version |

So `refused.policy` is `None` for the absent case, dimension 2 carries no
entitlement binding, and *which key was consulted* is recorded three ways that
the existing shape does hold — asserted by
`test_an_unregistered_tenants_denial_names_the_absence_and_pins_nothing`:

- the internal determination's `error_code` is `ERROR_PRODUCT_POLICY_ABSENT`;
- the internal `detail` names the key verbatim
  (`ccf.product-entitlement-policy/<tenant>`);
- `resource.tenant_id` names the tenant, and the key is a **pure derivation**
  from it (`product_entitlement_key` is a function, not a table) — the test
  asserts the derivation reproduces the key in the message.

The three refusals that *do* follow a resolution carry the real pin, so D-34's
*"under which policy"* is answered version-bearingly in every case where a
policy actually decided. See **F-1**.

## Findings for Dave

**F-1. The absent-policy pin needs a shape decision, and I took the
conservative one rather than inventing a fourth.** This is deviation 1's other
half, put here because the disposition is arguably yours.

When a tenant has **no** registered entitlement policy, the fabric consulted a
key and it resolved nothing. That is a real, positive fact about the
determination, and today it is recorded in the internal envelope's `error_code`,
in its `detail` (which names the key), and in the resource identity's tenant —
but **not** in replay dimension 2, because dimension 2's two shapes both refuse
a versionless artifact by construction and I would not weaken either.

**Three ways to close it, and I picked none:**

1. **Leave it as shipped.** The key is derivable from the recorded tenant by an
   SDK function, so nothing is lost — it is just derived rather than stated.
   Zero further work.
2. **A fourth `ProvenanceSource.kind`** — say `"consulted"` — meaning *this key
   was consulted and held nothing*. Honest, and arguably exactly in the spirit
   of amendment 2 (1) (it is the *anti*-fake-artifact), but it widens a closed
   vocabulary you shaped, and it touches `provenance.py`, `docs/schemas/`, the
   surface manifest and the refusal-vocabulary walk.
3. **Relax the `artifact`/version validator.** Cheapest in lines and worst in
   substance: it reopens the versionless *present* artifact pin, which is the
   "latest" hazard `D-IMPL-26` closed.

My reading is that (1) is right for this phase and (2) is worth considering when
something else needs it. It is a ruling, not a defect, and nothing is red.

**F-2. A `reasoned` disclosure crosses `policy_bindings`, and nobody has ruled
whether a Control Plane key and version may reach a consumer.** The plan
enumerates what `minimal` withholds — detail, fields, evidence, provenance,
lineage, cause, error_code — and `policy_bindings` is not on that list, so
`disclose` does not touch it at any level. That is what I implemented.

It means a refused consumer can read *which Control Plane artifacts governed the
refusal, at which versions*. For a caller inside the tenant that is probably
right and useful. Across an A2A boundary, or for a suppression-sensitive
subject, it is a real channel: two denials that pinned different policies are
distinguishable even at `minimal`.

It does not bite today — `govern_entitlement` only ever produces
`cause="unauthorized"`, and the nonexistent-subject case is produced by an
assembler — but the two will meet at one door in plans 09/10, and that is where
the control test for amendment 2 (4) lives. **Flagging it now** so the door
plans can be told which answer you want, rather than discovering the question at
close.

**F-3. `disclose` is total over the vocabulary, which means eight of
twenty-three kinds tell a consumer nothing but the kind at `minimal`.** Measured:
eight kinds have an empty `PERMITTED_AT_MINIMAL`, including every gate refusal
and both lineage refusals. That is deliberate — a gate's own measurement is
fabric state — but it is a visible change in what a consumer of a *gate* refusal

attestation refusal; what they lose at `minimal` is the numbers. If you would

table entry per kind and no code.
