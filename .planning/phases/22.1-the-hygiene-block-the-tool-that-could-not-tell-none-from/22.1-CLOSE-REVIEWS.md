### P3 — the decisive one, and it is the phase's own defect class

Cursor stated it as a mutation: *"remove `unexaminable=unexaminable()` from the
`Sweep` construction in `sweep()`."* Gemini reached the same place independently.

**Measured, exactly as they predicted.** Deleting that ONE line left **all 41
tests green** while the tool regressed completely — 261 rows at exit 0 under the
system interpreter, which is the original defect restored in full.

Every other test proved one of two disjoint things: that the RECORDER fires
(against `count_coupling_rows` and the module-level collector), or that `main()`
REFUSES (against a monkeypatched `sweep` returning a canned `Sweep`). Neither
touched the single line joining them. Cursor named the shape: *"guarded the
helper, not the wiring."*

This is `D-IMPL-188`'s open residual — *nothing asserts that the bounded
assertion actually READS the pin* — reproduced one module over, inside the phase
built to close a sibling of it. Now held by
`test_the_sweep_carries_the_examination_record_into_its_result`, with its own
false-positive control proving `sweep()` resets rather than accumulating.

### P10 — a finding I was about to reject, and the measurement said otherwise

Gemini said `test_control_28c` relies on the literal `"- [ ] **"` and could pass
vacuously if the ledger's bullet spelling changed. **I reasoned it would FAIL —
the control would fall through to its final `raise`.** I ran it rather than
recording the reasoning: under a simulated spelling change the control **passes**,
because `_an_unticked_id` refuses on the reformatted text (it finds no unticked
row *in the old spelling*), and the control returns early having "confirmed" a
refusal that fired for format drift rather than for an all-ticked ledger.

Gemini was right and my reasoning was wrong. A floor now asserts the ledger
carries an unticked bullet in the spelling the control mutates, and it is
verified to fire on the drift case. **Recorded because the near-miss is the
point: an unverified rejection is as bad as an unverified acceptance.**

### P11 — rejected, with the reason

`D-21` forbids a carried count in a **standing check over a live tree**, where a
literal must be re-edited whenever a row is minted and can be silently satisfied
by a restored total. The assertions cursor names (`len(recorded) == 1`,
`len(scans) == 1`, `len(healthy) == 2`) are over **synthetic fixtures the test
itself constructs three lines above**, in a module that carries no `D-21` gate.
The count is the fixture's own arity, not a measurement of the repository.
Changing them to rosters would add ceremony without adding a guard.

### P12 — rejected, refuted by measurement

Gemini predicted that `_an_unticked_id`'s `return unticked[0] if unticked else ""`
would make `CONTROL 6` fail for the wrong reason under a neutered limb, masking
the real cause. `test_control_35_every_limb_aimed_control_reaches_the_limb_it_names`
— the standing check that every limb-aimed control actually reaches its limb —
**passes**, which is the direct refutation. The `if unticked else ""` guard is
required for the opposite reason and is documented at the site: without it an
unguarded `unticked[0]` raises `IndexError` under neutering, and a crash is
indistinguishable from a caught defect to every coverage assertion in that module.

### P13 — confirmed correct, by all three independently

All three traced the polarity and reached the same answer: a trailing comment
missed by the fallback is classified `LIVE` rather than `DOCUMENTARY`, so the
degraded path **over**-reports a coupling, which is the safe direction for a
deletion tool. Cursor added a fair correction to the comment's wording — it is a
false *positive coupling*, not a "false red of the refusal" — and the comment was
already written in the accepted form.
