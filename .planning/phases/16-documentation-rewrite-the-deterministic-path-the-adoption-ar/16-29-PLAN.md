<objective>
Discharge `D-IMPL-134` and the derivation half of `D-IMPL-136`: turn a parsed
`DeploymentConfig` into a `DeploymentManifest` plus one materialised document per
section, applying design spec §6's derivation table and every refusal §6.1,
§11.1 and §11.2 demand.

Purpose: `manifest.yaml` is *"46% of the config surface and is an index of
itself"*. Deleting it means the loader must answer, for every section, the five
questions the index answered — `path`, `key`, `class_id`, `role`, `registration`
— and must answer them **without** softening the two rulings that made two of
them mandatory. `D-IMPL-134` narrows: derived where the SDK owns the class and
there is exactly one correct answer; **required, with no default, everywhere
else**, because that is where the hazard lives — an adopter accidentally
content-hashing event data into replay dimension 2.

This plan produces no side effects. Nothing is registered, nothing is booted, no
file is read except the one handed in. That is what makes the derivation
testable as a pure function and what lets `16-30` prove equivalence by comparing
two derivations rather than two side-effect traces.

Output: one module, two public names, two test modules, one surface fragment,
one regenerated `docs/SURFACE-CHANGES.md`.
</objective>
