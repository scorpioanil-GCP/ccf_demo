# 15.6-10 — the manifest learns to read a Protocol's `__call__`

## This is an audit change, not a surface change

Every entry below moved because **the tool can now see it**, not because the
code changed. No Protocol in this fragment gained, lost or reordered an
argument in this plan. Read this fragment as a *record catching up with
reality*, not as a wave of new breaks — with one exception, named in the next
section, which is a real break that shipped in Phase 15.5 and is only now
visible.

`D-IMPL-110` established the defect by demonstration rather than by argument.
`tools/dump_surface.py` resolved a class's shape with `inspect.signature` on
the **class**, which for a `Protocol` returns `__init__` — universally
`(*args, **kwargs)`. Plan `15.5-15` then widened both product-resolver seams
from three arguments to five, a break for every adopter implementing them, and
`docs/surface/v0.2-surface.json` was **byte-identical across that change**. A
manifest that cannot distinguish a three-argument Protocol from a
five-argument one was reporting a surface stability it had never verified, and
`D-GATE-15.5` had to record that caveat rather than cite the manifest.

Two plans hit the same wall independently — `15.5-11` for `ReplayEnvironment`
and `15.5-15` for the two resolvers — and both were forced to classify their
change `behavior` rather than `reshaped`, because `reshaped` fails
`tests/test_surface_completeness.py` when the manifest shows no diff line to
point at.

## The one entry an adopter must actually act on

Of everything below, **only the two product-resolver Protocols carry a real
migration**, and it is a break that already shipped:
`ccf_runtime.mcp.egress.ProductResolverProtocol` and
`ccf_runtime.mcp.experience.ExperienceProductResolverProtocol` went from three
arguments to five in Phase 15.5. A deployment still supplying a three-argument
resolver has been broken since then and this manifest could not say so. Their
entries are reclassified from `behavior` to `reshaped` in
`docs/surface-changes/15.5-15-resolver-seam.md` under the authorization
`D-IMPL-110` recorded, and the migration is restated in full there.

Everything else in this fragment has `Migration: none`. That is the point of
separating them: an audit diff of twenty-two lines can bury the one line that
matters, and grouping the mechanical entries under the two headings below is
what keeps the actionable one readable.

## The re-measured inventory, and its drift from the record

Measured at this plan's base commit `673668d`, reproducing the manifest rather
than quoting a prior count:

Of **84** manifest entries recording the information-free shape
`(*args, **kwargs)`, **60 are class-kind** (51 distinct classes, every one
confirmed `_is_protocol` — no false positives) and **24 are alias-kind** (20
distinct type aliases). **34** of the information-free entries are named
`*Protocol`. Of the 51 distinct Protocol classes, **exactly 12 define
`__call__`**.

Beside the two prior measurements, both of which this supersedes:

`D-IMPL-110` measured at `4d16cb9` — 56 information-free entries, 30 named
`*Protocol`, 54 Protocol classes. `D-IMPL-122` re-measured at `4b407e0` — 80
information-free entries (60 class-kind / 51 distinct classes, 20 alias-kind /
18 distinct aliases), 34 named `*Protocol`, 56 Protocol definition sites, 12
with `__call__`.

**The drift is real and it is worth stating as a finding.** The Protocol side
has not moved since `4b407e0` — still 60 entries, 51 distinct classes, 12 with
`__call__` — but the **alias side grew from 18 distinct / 20 entries to 20
distinct / 24 entries**, because plans `15.6-06` and `15.6-08` each introduced
an `Annotated` alias exported from two modules: `DebtEvidence` and
`SignalObligations`. `D-IMPL-122`'s figure was correct when measured and is
already stale two waves later, which is exactly why this plan re-derived rather
than planned against it. The load-bearing figure — 12 `__call__` Protocols of
which 11 are in the frozen baseline, therefore **11 new `reshaped` diff lines**
from the Protocol half — reproduced exactly.

Measured cost of the whole fix: **36 manifest entries move** (12 Protocol, 24
alias) producing **22 new `reshaped` diff lines** — 11 from the Protocol half
and 11 from the alias half; the remaining 14 moved entries are names the frozen
baseline never had, so they were already claimed as `added` by the fragments
that introduced them.

## The frozen baseline was not touched

`D-IMPL-122` limb 1 ruled `q2a-reconcile`: `docs/surface/v0.2-surface-baseline.json`
stays frozen and the diff is reconciled against it, entry by entry, rather than
the baseline being regenerated to make the diff smaller. That is why this
fragment carries twenty mechanical entries. Regenerating the baseline would
have bought a shorter fragment at the price of making the already-shipped
resolver break invisible again — the opposite of the audit `DI-15.5-06` exists
to produce. Neither the baseline file nor the `_BASELINE_DIGEST` constant in
`tests/test_surface_completeness.py` is modified by this plan.

## What this fix deliberately leaves open

**After this fix the surface manifest is still not evidence of surface
stability for a multi-method Protocol, and must not be cited as such at any
gate.** `D-IMPL-122` limb 2 scoped the fix to `__call__` capture and declined
full method-set capture on a measured cost — 65 new `reshaped` diff lines
against 11. `__call__` capture reaches **12 of the 51** information-free
Protocol classes. The other **39 remain information-free**: their entire public
contract is a method set, `__call__` capture reaches none of them, and a
signature change to any of their methods still produces no diff line at all.

Every one of the seven Protocols `D-IMPL-110` named by name is in the descoped
39 — `ArtifactStoreProtocol`, `ControlPlaneProtocol`, `HarnessAdapterProtocol`,
`OrchestrationProtocol`, `HITLTransportProtocol`, `LLMClientProtocol`,
`DebtFactorScorerProtocol` — that is, most of the pluggable substrate
CLAUDE.md §11 mandates Protocols for. So are `GraphReader`, `GraphWriter`,
`Operator`, `ArchetypeComponent`, `PeeringProtocol` and
`ccf.replay.engine.ReplayEnvironment`. That last one is why `15.5-11`'s entry
is **not** reclassified in this plan: the reclassification `D-IMPL-110`
anticipated for it needs the method-set fix that was descoped, so its
`behavior` classification remains correct and remains the only honest one
available. `ccf.graph.reader.Row` has zero members and has nothing to show
under any fix.

The descope is asserted, not merely written down:
`tests/test_surface_shape_capture.py::test_a_protocol_with_no_call_keeps_the_information_free_shape`
pins the no-`__call__` behaviour against three of the seven, so closing the
remaining blind spot is a deliberate scope change with a ruling behind it
rather than a silent one. `DI-15.5-06` is **narrowed, not closed**.

## The type aliases are in scope (`D-IMPL-122` limb 3)

`q2b-in`. All 20 distinct information-free aliases now record their rendered
type. Eleven of them produce a new `reshaped` diff line and carry entries
below; the other nine are names the frozen baseline never had, already claimed
as `added` by the fragments that introduced them, and their shape moving adds
no diff line.

Worth recording because a carried figure was wrong in a way that would have
scoped the work incorrectly: the prior framing described "five `Literal` type
aliases". Measured, five is exactly the count of the **non**-`Literal` ones.
`D-IMPL-122` recorded that correction; this plan reproduced it and found two
more aliases besides.

**A determinism trap the alias half walks into and had to be closed.**
`str()` of an `Annotated` alias carrying a Pydantic validator renders as
`<function name at 0x...>`. Committed verbatim, that would make two runs on an
unchanged tree emit different manifests — a worse defect than the blind spot
being fixed, and one that would have surfaced as an unreproducible `make
surface` diff rather than as a clean failure. The renderer scrubs the address
and keeps the validator's name, and
`tests/test_surface_shape_capture.py::test_the_serialized_manifest_carries_no_memory_addresses`
asserts the property over the whole serialized manifest so a future alias form
that leaks one fails loudly.

## The tooling change itself

## tools/dump_surface.py

- Surface: tools/dump_surface.py
- Public surface: false
- Change type: behavior
- Before: `_shape_of` resolved every class with `inspect.signature` on the
  class, returning `__init__` — `(*args, **kwargs)` for every Protocol — and
  applied the same signature rendering to type aliases
- After: a Protocol's own `__call__` is resolved through the MRO and rendered
  with `self` elided; a type alias renders its type, scrubbed of the
  process-local memory address an `Annotated` alias would otherwise embed;
  everything else is untouched
- Why: `DI-15.5-06`, ruled by `D-IMPL-122`. The manifest could not see the
  three-to-five argument widening `15.5-15` shipped, so it was reporting a
  surface stability it had never verified
- Migration: none for adopters. Maintainers should expect `make surface` to
  produce a one-time 36-entry reshape on first run after this change, and
  should read it as the tool's field of view widening rather than as the
  surface moving

## tests/test_surface_shape_capture.py

- Surface: tests/test_surface_shape_capture.py
- Public surface: false
- Change type: added
- Before: absent — `dump_surface` had no dedicated test module, and what it
  could and could not see about a shape was pinned nowhere
- After: fifteen assertions covering the capture, the structural detection, the
  alias rendering, manifest determinism, the narrowness of the change, and the
  descope
- Why: a descope that is not asserted is a descope that quietly becomes a
  regression. The module lives in `tests/` rather than beside the tool because
  `pyproject.toml` sets `testpaths = ["tests"]`, so a module under `tools/` runs
  only in CI's separate step and not under `make ci-core` — the wrong gate for a
  change to the surface record's own generator
- Migration: none
