# 15.6-13 — the freshness signal, without the source identities

## This revises a change from earlier in the same phase

**Read this before the entries below: it is a correction, not a second
feature.** An adopter who read
`docs/surface-changes/15.6-07-source-refresh-recency.md` has already been told
that `ContextProductContract` carries source-refresh recency. It still does.
What changed is **what that member carries**.

**What plan 15.6-07 shipped, nine plans ago.** A per-source map:
`source_refresh_recency` held a tuple of `SourceRefresh` entries, each pairing
a `source_ref: str` — an opaque-to-the-SDK identifier declared by the
deployment — with a `refreshed_at: datetime`. It shipped correctly against the
instructions it had: `D-IMPL-119` governs evidence pointers rather than refresh
timestamps, and 15.6-07 was told not to apply its limbs to this field by
analogy. It did not.

**What that plan's executor then named rather than ruled.**
`15.6-07-SUMMARY.md` Findings § 3, verbatim: *"a reader could learn that a
source **exists** and **when it last published** without being entitled to its
contents … This is a new question, not a limb of an existing entry, and it is
not ruled here."* Reporting it rather than disposing of it silently is the
behaviour the checkpoint exists to elicit.

**Dave's ruling on it, verbatim and in full** (`D-IMPL-124`, 2026-08-12):

> *"Source-set / recency: the field's purpose is freshness, not source
> enumeration. Publish the freshness signal without human-interpretable source
> identities (Shape B). The inference channel is closed rather than accepted as
> residual."*

**This is the third instance of one inference-channel shape in this project,
and all three are closed.** `D-IMPL-105` closed it for **completeness**;
`D-IMPL-119` closed it for **evidence pointers**; `D-IMPL-124` closes it for
**source recency**. The word that matters in each is *closed*.

**Shape C is the recorded long-term design, and it is deliberately not built
here.** Opaque tokens on the product, with resolution to a human-interpretable
reference only behind an entitlement-checked door, is in Dave's own words *"the
right long-term design but is new work."* `D-IMPL-124` records it as **NOT
TAKEN** and names it as the long-term shape, so a later phase adding a
resolution door is completing a design already on the record rather than
re-opening a closed one. This plan builds no token, no resolution seam and no
door.

## What this closes, and what it does not

**What it closes: the identity channel.** Both shipped delivery surfaces
serialize the whole vessel verbatim —
`runtime/ccf_runtime/mcp/egress.py::build_egress_server` returns
`canonical_bytes(servable.product)` and
`runtime/ccf_runtime/http_api/routes/product.py::build_router` returns
`canonical_response(servable.product)` — and nothing in the assembly path is
principal-scoped. So a `source_ref` on the vessel reached every principal who
could reach the product. After this plan, a consumer **cannot** learn that a
system called `name-screening` exists, and **cannot** learn when that system in
particular last published. The `source_ref` field is gone, and the class that
carried it is deleted rather than deprecated, so the shape is unavailable
rather than merely unused.

**It also closes the cardinality residue.** The window carries no count and no
`sources_summarized` figure. A count is source enumeration in aggregate — it
discloses how many systems a deployment reads while naming none of them — and
it is not freshness, so it is outside what the field exists for.

**What it does not claim.** Two instants are still information. A reader still
learns that *some* source published at the oldest end of the window and *some*
source published at the newest. This fragment does not assert that no inference
whatever remains — it asserts that the **identity** channel the ruling names is
closed, which is the same standard `D-IMPL-105` and `D-IMPL-119` each met: each
closed a channel by removing what was interpretable, not by asserting that
nothing could ever be inferred.

The channel is **closed**. It was not waved through, it is not being lived
with, and it is not carried forward as a risk somebody has decided to bear. No
later reader should find it recorded as one.

## Why `oldest`/`newest` and not `stalest`/`freshest`

`D-IMPL-124` offers both spellings. This plan chose `oldest_source_refresh_at`
and `newest_source_refresh_at`, and the reason is discipline rather than taste:
*stale* and *fresh* are **judgement words**. A member named `sources_stalest_at`
asserts that the SDK holds a notion of staleness, which `D-IMPL-117` says it
owes nothing of — *"owing no definition of a source or a cadence"* — and which
`D-IMPL-101` deferred as salience. *Oldest* and *newest* describe the ordering
of two declared instants and assert nothing about whether either is acceptable.

Two members rather than one, because a single instant cannot express the span:
with sources on different cadences, a lone "newest" hides the oldest and a lone
"oldest" hides the newest, and `D-IMPL-112` gap 2's whole complaint was that a
reader could not tell how old the underlying filings were.

## Where the reduction lives, and why not in the SDK

`examples/s1-01-hello-context-product/artifacts/product-governance.yaml` still
declares its three per-source entries with their human-interpretable
references. **Those references stop at the deployment boundary.**
`deployment.py::_declared_refresh_window` reads them, reduces them with `min`
and `max` over the declared instants, and the window is what crosses onto the
product.

The SDK ships **no reducer**. A `SourceRefreshWindow.from_sources(...)` helper
— or any `src/` function taking per-source instants — would put a
source-bearing input type back on the public surface, and would make the SDK
the party deciding which of a deployment's sources count toward the window.
Whether a deployment's set is *complete* is a fact only that deployment can
vouch for, which is exactly why the reduction is the deployment's work.

## The two withdrawn entries, and the measurement behind the withdrawal

Plan 15.6-07 filed `added` entries for
`ccf.halflife.assessment.SourceRefresh` and its `ccf.halflife.debt` re-export
spelling. This plan **deletes** that class — and
`docs/surface/v0.2-surface.json` has not been regenerated since before plan
15.6-06, because plan 15.6-10 owns this phase's single regeneration.

So the branch was **measured before it was taken**, not assumed. Diffing the
live surface against the frozen Phase-15 baseline
(`docs/surface/v0.2-surface-baseline.json`, the immutable before-side the
completeness gate uses):

| Name | Partition |
| --- | --- |
| `ccf.halflife.assessment.SourceRefresh` | **absent from the diff entirely** — neither `added` nor `removed` |
| `ccf.halflife.debt.SourceRefresh` | **absent from the diff entirely** — neither `added` nor `removed` |

A name added and withdrawn inside one unregenerated manifest window never
became public surface at all. So **both** 15.6-07's `added` claim and a naive
`removed` claim here would be entries with no matching diff line, which is
exactly what `tests/test_surface_completeness.py::test_every_public_log_entry_has_a_matching_diff_line`
fails on.

**The branch taken:** the two entries are withdrawn from
`docs/surface-changes/15.6-07-source-refresh-recency.md`, replaced there by a
comment recording the withdrawal rather than deleted silently, and **no
`removed` entry is filed here** for a name that was never published. The
history survives in
`.planning/phases/15.6-decision-completeness-and-defect-closure/15.6-07-SUMMARY.md`,
which is not edited.

## ccf.halflife.assessment.SourceRefreshWindow

- Surface: ccf.halflife.assessment.SourceRefreshWindow
- Public surface: true
- Before: absent
- After: a `CCFContract` with exactly two fields —
  `oldest_source_refresh_at: datetime` and
  `newest_source_refresh_at: datetime`, the earliest and latest of the refresh
  instants the assembling deployment declared. Both are timezone-aware; a naive
  instant on either is **refused at construction** rather than coerced. One
  coherence validator refuses a window whose oldest end is after its newest;
  equality is legal, because one source — or several that published together —
  is a window of zero span. No `source_ref`, no count, no cadence, no staleness
  threshold and no default
- Change type: added
- Why: `D-IMPL-124` Shape B. It replaces the per-source map plan 15.6-07
  shipped, because the field's purpose is freshness rather than source
  enumeration and a per-source map published the deployment's inventory of
  systems to every principal who could reach the product. The
  oldest-versus-newest check is **not** the comparison `D-IMPL-101` deferred:
  the forbidden comparisons are against `now`, against a threshold and against
  a cadence, each of which draws a conclusion about whether a value is
  acceptable; this one checks that a single declared fact is internally
  coherent, exactly as the duplicate-reference refusal it replaces did
- Migration: none — new name. Note that the SDK compares these instants to
  nothing outside themselves, so if your deployment wants "are my sources
  overdue?", that comparison is yours to write against your own cadence. Note
  also that the SDK ships **no reducer** from per-source instants to a window:
  that reduction belongs in your deployment, because only your deployment knows
  its set of sources is complete

## ccf.halflife.assessment.SourceRefreshRecency

- Surface: ccf.halflife.assessment.SourceRefreshRecency
- Public surface: true
- Before: the annotated carrier type plan 15.6-07 shipped — a
  `tuple[SourceRefresh, ...]` validated to be source-unique and canonically
  ordered
- After: `SourceRefreshWindow | None`. The ordering and uniqueness validators
  are gone with the collection: a two-field contract has no declaration order,
  so there is nothing to canonicalise
- Change type: reshaped
- Why: `D-IMPL-124`. **Replace, not parallel** — the ruling's operative
  sentence is *"no `source_ref` strings leave the vessel"* and *"the inference
  channel is closed"*, and a parallel shape would leave the per-source map on
  the vessel, which is the rejected Shape A wearing a second field. It keeps
  its **name** for `ccf.halflife.assessment.DebtEvidence`'s recorded reason:
  the carrier lands in **two** places, the vessel and replay dimension 5, and
  one named shape is what keeps those two from drifting apart
- Migration: nothing is required to keep constructing products — the sentinel
  default covers every existing site. An adopter who **populated** this alias
  under 15.6-07 supplies a `SourceRefreshWindow` instead of a
  `tuple[SourceRefresh, ...]`; the change on your side is the one-line
  reduction `min`/`max` over the instants you were already declaring

## ccf.halflife.assessment.NO_SOURCE_REFRESH_RECENCY

- Surface: ccf.halflife.assessment.NO_SOURCE_REFRESH_RECENCY
- Public surface: true
- Before: the empty tuple, as a named module-level constant
- After: `None`, under the **same name** and making the **same positive claim**
  — *this product records nothing about when its sources last refreshed*
- Change type: reshaped
- Why: the value moved because `()` is the empty case of a *collection*, and a
  window is not a collection. There is no empty window that does not require
  inventing an instant, and an invented instant is the silent default
  `D-IMPL-38` forbids. The same-vessel precedent is exact:
  `ContextProductContract.debt` and `.request` are both optional *structures*
  whose `None` is documented as a positive assertion in the same voice. What
  the **name** buys, and why it is kept rather than replaced by a bare `None`
  at each site, is unchanged: every construction site and every default still
  spells the claim, so a deployment that knows nothing about its sources'
  publication schedule and one whose recency somebody dropped in transit remain
  different artifacts
- Migration: none for construction — it remains the default on both fields that
  carry the alias, so every existing construction site asserts exactly what it
  always asserted. If your code compared this constant to `()`, compare it to
  `None`, or better, compare against the named constant

## ccf.products.contract.ContextProductContract

- Surface: ccf.products.contract.ContextProductContract
- Public surface: true
- Before: `source_refresh_recency` carried a source-unique, canonically ordered
  tuple of `SourceRefresh` entries, each naming a source and an instant
- After: **the same field name**, carrying a `SourceRefreshWindow | None`. The
  field count is unchanged; no member is added and none is removed
- Change type: reshaped
- Why: `D-IMPL-124`. The vessel is what both shipped delivery paths serialize
  verbatim to every principal who can reach the product, so the vessel is
  precisely where a source identity must not be. `D-IMPL-118`'s `qa-toplevel`
  placement is unamended — this is a change of contents, not of placement
- Migration: **nothing is required to keep constructing products.** The
  sentinel default keeps every existing construction site saying exactly what
  it always said, and the field name did not move, so code that reads
  `product.source_refresh_recency` still compiles. **An adopter who populated
  the field under 15.6-07 must now supply a window instead of a per-source
  tuple** — a one-line reduction on your side, `min`/`max` over the instants
  you already declare. Anything of yours that read a `source_ref` off a
  delivered product must instead read it from your own deployment
  configuration, which is where `D-IMPL-124` rules that knowledge belongs

## ccf.replay.engine.ReplayEnvironment

- Surface: ccf.replay.engine.ReplayEnvironment
- Public surface: true
- Before: `rehydrate_product`'s contract required the per-source tuple to be
  read off `record.content_generation_path.source_refresh_recency` and carried
  onto the rebuilt product verbatim
- After: **the same signature**, and the same requirement over the reshaped
  value. A conforming implementation copies the window exactly as it copied the
  map
- Change type: behavior
- Why: the Protocol's contract text is where the read-back obligation is
  stated, and the obligation's *subject* changed type even though its
  *signature* did not — which is the class of change a signature diff cannot
  see, and the reason the `behavior` change type exists
- Migration: **none — the read-back line is unchanged in spelling** and is
  required exactly as before. Its necessity was re-measured against the
  reshaped fixture in both directions: deleting it over a sentinel-carrying
  fixture costs nothing (0 red), and over a populated window it turns 13 of
  `tests/replay/test_engine_rehydration.py`'s 44 cases red with
  `ReplayDivergence`. Both figures are recorded in a comment at the read-back
  line, because reporting only the populated one would hide the reason the
  measurement is taken twice
